# Sources for An Accidental Guide to… Your First D&D Game

All fetched 22 October 2026 (raw pages saved outside the repo while writing). Excerpts are
verbatim and short. Public, official material from the game's publisher only. Nothing
internal. No text from the SRD or the Basic Rules is copied into the guide beyond short facts
restated in plain words.

## 1. D&D Beyond Basic Rules (2024), Playing the Game

Wizards of the Coast, D&D Beyond Basic Rules, "Playing the Game".
<https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game>
Page date: posted Sep 1, 2024. Fetched 22 October 2026.

Roles, the DM:

- One DM and several players: "To play D&D, you need one person to be the Dungeon Master and
  other players (three to six are best) to play adventurers."
- The DM isn't your opponent: "Although the DM controls all the monsters you encounter, the
  DM isn't your adversary."
- What DMs do: "Guide the Story. You narrate much of the action during play, describing
  locations and creatures that the adventurers face." and "Adjudicate the Rules. You oversee
  how the group uses the game's rules, making sure the rules serve the group's fun."
- Players: "Make a Character. Your character is your alter ego in the fantasy world of the
  game." "Team Up. Your character joins the other players' characters to form an adventuring
  party."

The rhythm of play (the loop):

- "The Dungeon Master Describes a Scene. The DM tells the players where their adventurers
  are and what's around them"
- "The Players Describe What Their Characters Do."
- "The DM Narrates the Results of the Adventurers' Actions. ... In those cases, the DM might
  ask the player to roll a die to help determine what happens. Describing the results often
  leads to another decision point, which brings the game back to step 1."
- "In certain situations—particularly combat—the action is more structured, and everyone
  takes turns."
- Session, one-shot, campaign: "Sometimes a session of D&D is a complete game in itself
  (often called a "one-shot")" ... "adventures can be strung together into a larger
  narrative called a campaign."

Dice:

- "The dice used in D&D are referred to by the letter d followed by the die's number of
  sides: d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and d20. For instance, a d6 is a six-sided die"
- Notation: "“3d8 + 5” means you roll three eight-sided dice, add them together, and add 5
  to the total."
- Percentile: "the common way to roll 1d100 uses a pair of ten-sided dice numbered from 0 to
  9, known as percentile dice."
- The d20: "The 20-sided die (d20) is the most important die you'll use in the game." "You
  roll a d20 whenever your character tries to do something that the DM decides has a chance
  of both success and failure. The higher your roll, the more likely it is that you succeed."
- Damage dice: "The most common use for dice other than the d20 is to determine damage."
  "For example, a Dagger uses 1d4, a Greataxe uses 1d12, and a Fireball spell uses 8d6."
- Interpreting rolls: "If you're a player, look for opportunities to interpret your
  character's die rolls and describe why things unfolded unexpectedly or even humorously."

D20 Tests:

- "These rolls are called D20 Tests, and they come in three kinds: ability checks, saving
  throws, and attack rolls."
- "Roll 1d20. You always want to roll high."
- "Add Modifiers." (the relevant ability modifier, the Proficiency Bonus if relevant, and
  circumstantial bonuses)
- "If the total of the d20 and its modifiers equals or exceeds the target number, the D20
  Test succeeds. Otherwise, it fails. The Dungeon Master determines target numbers and tells
  players whether their rolls are successful. The target number for an ability check or a
  saving throw is called a Difficulty Class (DC). The target number for an attack roll is
  called an Armor Class (AC), which appears on a character sheet"
- Natural 20 and natural 1, attack rolls only: "If you roll a 20 on the d20 (called a
  “natural 20”) for an attack roll, the attack hits regardless of any modifiers or the
  target's AC." "If you roll a 1 on the d20 (a “natural 1”) for an attack roll, the attack
  misses regardless of any modifiers or the target's AC."
- Advantage: "When a roll has either Advantage or Disadvantage, roll a second d20 when you
  make the roll. Use the higher of the two rolls if you have Advantage, and use the lower roll
  if you have Disadvantage."
- Saving throws: "A saving throw—also called a save—represents an attempt to evade or
  resist a threat" ... "You don't normally choose to make a save; you must make one because
  your character or a monster (if you're the DM) is at risk."

Turns:

- "A round represents about 6 seconds in the game world."
- "When combat starts, every participant rolls Initiative; they make a Dexterity check that
  determines their place in the Initiative order."
- "On your turn, you can move a distance up to your Speed and take one action. You decide
  whether to move first or take your action first."
- Actions list includes Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence, Magic, Ready,
  Search, Study, Utilize: "Help another creature's ability check or attack roll, or
  administer first aid."
- Improvising: "When you describe an action not detailed elsewhere in the rules, the Dungeon
  Master tells you whether that action is possible and what kind of D20 Test you need to
  make, if any."
- Talking is free: "You can communicate however you are able—through brief utterances and
  gestures—as you take your turn. Doing so uses neither your action nor your move."
- One free object: "You can interact with one object or feature of the environment for
  free, during either your move or action."
- Bonus Action only if a feature gives one: "You can take a Bonus Action only when a special
  ability, a spell, or another feature of the game states that you can do something as a
  Bonus Action. You otherwise don't have a Bonus Action to take."
- Reaction: "A Reaction is an instant response to a trigger of some kind, which can occur on
  your turn or on someone else's."
- Doing nothing (from SRD 5.2.1, same wording): "If you can't decide what to do, consider
  taking the defensive Dodge action or the Ready action to delay acting."

Playing well:

- "Ask yourself as you play, “What would my character do?”"
- "avoid character choices that ruin the fun of the other players and the DM. Choose actions
  that delight you and your friends."

## 2. D&D Beyond Basic Rules (2024), Creating a Character

Wizards of the Coast, D&D Beyond Basic Rules, "Creating a Character".
<https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/creating-a-character>
Page date: posted Sep 1, 2024 (the page's own "Posted by … Sep 1, 2024" line).

- "we use the term “character sheet” to mean whatever you use to track your character's
  details, whether it's a printed character sheet, a digital character sheet like the one
  on D&D Beyond, or plain paper."
- Proficiency Bonus "is +2 for a level 1 character".
- "Typically, a character starts at level 1"
- "Your class and Constitution modifier determine your Hit Point maximum at level 1"
- "The character sheet includes room to note your current Hit Points when you take damage"
- "Initiative. Write your Dexterity modifier in the space for Initiative on your character
  sheet."
- "Without armor or a shield, your base Armor Class is 10 plus your Dexterity modifier."
- "Melee attack bonus = Strength modifier + Proficiency Bonus"
- "Ranged attack bonus = Dexterity modifier + Proficiency Bonus"
- "Your character's size and Speed are determined by the character's species"
- Skills: "For skills you have proficiency in, add your Proficiency Bonus to the ability
  modifier associated with that skill, and note the total."

## 3. System Reference Document 5.2.1

Wizards of the Coast, System Reference Document 5.2.1, CC BY 4.0.
<https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf>
Page date: the PDF's own dates are 23 and 29 April 2025.

- License: "provided to you free of charge by Wizards of the Coast LLC (“Wizards”) under the
  terms of the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License"
- The SRD's name for the DM: "1: The Game Master Describes a Scene. The GM tells the players
  where their adventurers are"
- Six abilities: Strength "Physical might", Dexterity "Agility, reflexes, and balance",
  Constitution "Health and stamina", Intelligence "Reasoning and memory", Wisdom
  "Perceptiveness and mental fortitude", Charisma "Confidence, poise, and charm".
- "Each ability has a score from 1 to 20, although some monsters have a score as high as 30."
  "10–11 This represents the human average."
- Modifiers: "Each ability has a modifier that you apply whenever you make a D20 Test with
  that ability". Table: 10–11 → +0, 12–13 → +1, 14–15 → +2, 16–17 → +3, 8–9 → −1.
- Ability check examples: "Dexterity Move nimbly, quickly, or quietly" (Ability Check
  Examples table), used for the sneaking example.
- Dodge (Actions table, same in the Basic Rules): "Until the start of your next turn, attack
  rolls against you have Disadvantage, and you make Dexterity saving throws with Advantage."
- Bonus Action: "you take an additional action on your turn called a Bonus Action"
- Typical DCs: "Very easy 5", "Easy 10", "Medium 15", "Hard 20", "Very hard 25", "Nearly
  impossible 30".
- Hit Points: "Whenever you take damage, subtract it from your Hit Points." "Hit Point loss
  has no effect on your capabilities until you reach 0 Hit Points."
- Doing nothing on your turn: "You can forgo moving, taking an action, or doing anything at
  all on your turn. If you can't decide what to do, consider taking the defensive Dodge
  action or the Ready action to delay acting."
- Not stated: the SRD and Basic Rules don't say how long a session lasts.

## 5. D&D Beyond, Playing D&D for the First Time? Here Are Some Tips

D&D Beyond post, Jan 18, 2018 (a video interview with one of the game's designers; the
advice isn't tied to a rules edition). Practitioner advice from the publisher's site, marked
as such. <https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/139-playing-d-d-for-the-first-time-here-are-some-tips>

- "You're not required to know every D&D rule on your very first game."
- "just know generally what your character can do"
- "bring your dice, bring a writing instrument, bring some scrap paper"
- "If you're in combat, think about what you want your character to do before it's your
  turn."
- "Make listening your first priority and speaking your second priority. Give everyone a
  chance to shine"
- "be considerate of others, and also take actions and play into the group as a whole"
- "D&D's about having fun taking on challenges in the fantasy world" ... "you're not there
  to win"
- Complaint it answers: "when it's someone's turn to do something, and they have no idea what
  they want to do, or they have to keep looking everything up."

## Where sources disagree

- The SRD 5.2.1 calls the role the Game Master (GM); the Basic Rules and the How to Play
  page say Dungeon Master (DM). The guide says Dungeon Master (DM), the word the reader will
  hear at the table, and notes once that the rules also say Game Master.
- Some groups still play the 2014 rules. The guide's facts are from the 2024 Basic Rules
  and SRD 5.2.1. The parts used (dice, the d20 roll, one action and a move) are the same
  shape in both, but the guide says "as of October 2026" and doesn't claim that.
