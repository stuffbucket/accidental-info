# Why is my smoke<br/>alarm chirping? {#why}

One short chirp every 30 to 60 seconds or so means a smoke alarm wants attention, not that there’s a fire. The usual cause is a low battery.

A weak battery struggles as the house cools, and most homes are coolest between 2 a.m. and 6 a.m. That’s why the chirp so often starts in the middle of the night.

> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | Chirp | A single short beep that asks for attention. |
> | Indicator light | The small light, or LED, whose blinking shows the smoke alarm’s condition. |
> | Battery | Main power, or backup power in a hardwired smoke alarm. |
> | Hush button | Quiets the smoke alarm for a while without fixing anything. |
> | Manufacture date | When the smoke alarm was made. Replace it 10 years later. |

# Which one is it? {#fit}

A chirp is hard to place by ear. The smoke alarm with the problem may blink differently, so use your eyes as well.

> | Step | What to do |
> |---|---|
> | Wait | Stand still until the next chirp. A minute can feel long at night. |
> | Look | Check each indicator light. The one that needs attention may blink differently from the rest. |
> | Narrow it down | Move room by room between chirps until the sound is loudest, then look at that smoke alarm’s light. |
> | Check the date | If it’s hardwired, turn off its breaker. Then take that smoke alarm down and read the date on the back. |

> [!WARNING]
> Never take out the battery or disconnect a smoke alarm to stop a chirp. A smoke alarm with a dead or missing battery is the same as none at all.

# Chirp {#entry num=01 mono=Cr}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A short, high beep with a long pause. |
| What | A chirp is the smoke alarm asking for attention. A low battery is the usual cause, and other patterns vary by model. |
| So what | A chirp that stops by morning can return the next night. Change the battery anyway. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Found on | One smoke alarm, usually |
> | Fixed by | Battery or smoke alarm |
> | Cannot | Warn you of smoke |

## Confused with: Alarm sound

A chirp is one short beep with a long pause. The alarm sound is loud and repeating, and means get out.

# Indicator light {#entry num=02 mono=Ld}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A small light on the cover, often red or green. |
| What | An indicator light shows the smoke alarm’s condition. Some models blink a special pattern when the battery is low. |
| So what | When you can’t place the chirp, compare the lights. The odd one out may be the one to fix. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Found on | The front cover |
> | Fixed by | Fixing what it shows |
> | Cannot | Mean the same on every model |

## Confused with: Standby blink

A standby blink is a regular flash, on some models every 40 seconds, that means all is well. Compare the lights side by side.

# Battery {#entry num=03 mono=Ba}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A battery under a door or the cover, of the type on the label. |
| What | A battery powers the smoke alarm, or backs up a hardwired one. Change it at least once a year. |
| So what | A new battery usually stops the chirp. Some smoke alarms keep chirping until they’re reset. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Found on | Under the cover |
> | Fixed by | A new battery |
> | Cannot | Fix an old smoke alarm |

## Confused with: Sealed battery

A replaceable battery comes out. A sealed battery lasts the smoke alarm’s whole life, so when one chirps, replace the smoke alarm.

# Hush button {#entry num=04 mono=Hu}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A button marked Hush or Silence, often the test button too. |
| What | A hush button quiets a nuisance alarm, such as cooking smoke, for a few minutes. On some models it also quiets a low-battery chirp for some hours. |
| So what | A hush may buy a night’s sleep but fixes nothing. The chirp comes back. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Found on | The cover |
> | Fixed by | Battery or smoke alarm |
> | Cannot | Remove the cause |

## Confused with: Test button

Many smoke alarms use one button for both. Press it during a chirp to hush, and at other times to test, at least once a month.

# Manufacture date {#entry num=05 mono=Md}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A date printed on the back or outside. |
| What | The manufacture date is when the smoke alarm was made. Sensors wear out, so replace it 10 years after that date. |
| So what | An old smoke alarm can be slow to sense a fire. If there’s no date, replace it now. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Found on | The back or outside |
> | Fixed by | A new smoke alarm |
> | Cannot | Be reset |

## Confused with: Battery

The manufacture date never changes. A replaceable battery wears out much sooner.

# What does the sound mean? {#apart}

> | Sound | Means | Do this |
> |---|---|---|
> | One chirp about every 30 to 60 seconds | Usually a low battery | Replace the battery, then press test. |
> | Chirping after a new battery | End of life, or a stored fault | Reset it: breaker off if hardwired, battery out, hold test 15 seconds, then put it back. Still chirping means replace it. |
> | Three long beeps repeating | Smoke or fire | Get out, call 9-1-1 and stay out. |
> | Four beeps repeating on a combination alarm | Carbon monoxide | Go outside, call 9-1-1 and stay out. |

Other patterns vary by model, so check the label or manual.
