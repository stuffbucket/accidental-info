## Why is my smoke alarm chirping? (lines 13 to 23)

2. Line 13: One short chirp every 30 to 60 seconds or so means a smoke alarm wants attention, and there’s no fire. The usual cause is a low battery. If a new battery doesn’t stop it, the smoke alarm is at the end of its life.

## Chirp

11. Line 44: | What | A chirp is the smoke alarm asking for attention. A low battery is the usual cause, and other patterns vary by model. |
12. Line 45: | So what | A chirp that stops by morning will be back the next night. Change the battery anyway. |
13. Line 57: A chirp is one short beep with a long pause. The alarm sound is loud and repeating, and means get out.

14. Line 64: | What | An indicator light shows the smoke alarm’s condition. Some models blink a special pattern when the battery is low. |

## Battery

18. Line 83: | Identify by | A 9-volt or AA battery under a door or the cover. |
19. Line 84: | What | A battery powers the smoke alarm, or backs up a hardwired one. Replace it once a year with the type on the label. |
20. Line 85: | So what | A new battery usually stops the chirp. If not, take it out, hold the test button for at least 15 seconds, and put it back to clear the old fault. |
21. Line 97: A replaceable battery comes out. A sealed battery lasts the smoke alarm’s whole life, so when one chirps, replace the smoke alarm.

## Hush button

22. Line 104: | What | A hush button quiets a nuisance alarm, such as cooking smoke, for a few minutes. On many models it also quiets a low-battery chirp for some hours. |
23. Line 105: | So what | A hush buys you a night’s sleep and fixes nothing. The chirp comes back. |
24. Line 117: Many smoke alarms use one button for both. Press it during a chirp to hush, and at other times to test, at least once a month.

28. Line 137: The manufacture date never changes. A battery wears out much sooner.
