# Review 1

Boards: all five match their FEN, view, marks and arrows, and each caption’s chess holds. Problems are in the text.

## Findings, most serious first

1. **L125, Wrong for this board.** “Move the piece to safety or defend it, here with 4.Nf3.” Defending a knight a pawn attacks (4.d4) still loses it for a pawn (glossary: “a piece attacked by a less valuable attacker”). Rewrite: “Move the knight to safety with 4.Nf3. Defending it with 4.d4 doesn’t help, because a pawn that takes a knight wins material even if you take back.”

2. **L71, Overreach.** “…the pieces they attack can’t capture them back in the same way.” Vague, and a forked knight can take back. Source: “cannot be captured by the non-knight pieces they attack”. Rewrite: “Knights fork well because no piece a knight attacks can capture it, except another knight.”

3. **L132, mismatch.** “Check the landing square”. 3.Nxe5 landed safely, and 3…d6 made it hang. Chess.com: “looking to see if it is protected.” Rewrite: “Check what is unprotected”.

4. **L89, caption vs board.** “White’s three pawns leave the king nowhere to go.” Reader sees f1 and h1 empty. Rewrite: “Black threatens Re1. The pawns on f2, g2 and h2 block the king, and the rook would also cover f1 and h1.”

5. **L143, reader.** “After 6.cxd4 Bb4+”. Can’t be replayed, unlike the other boards. Rewrite: “After 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb4+”.

6. **L150–151, board mismatch.** “…while the center files open” and “exposed on the center files”. The check runs along a diagonal, and dropping Wikipedia Castling leaves “center files” unsourced. Rewrite L150: “Your king still on e1 or e8 with open lines toward it.” L151: “A king in the center is an uncastled king facing open lines. Here the pawns from c3 and d2 have gone, so the bishop on b4 reaches e1. Castling moves the king away and brings a rook into the game.”

7. **L152, jargon.** “a developing move” is undefined. Rewrite: “Block the check with 7.Bd2 or 7.Nc3, which also bring a piece into play, then castle.”

8. **L17, reader.** “4…Bd7”, “Qxc2” and “Bb4+” stop a reader who knows only the moves. Add: “In 4…Bd7 the dots mark Black’s move, x marks a capture and + marks check.”

9. **L44, L50, Naming.** “Break the line”. Sources say “block the line”, and “break” doesn’t tell the reader what to do. Rewrite L44: “Block the line with 4…Bd7, putting the bishop between the knight and the king. In other positions you can also move the king, capture the bishop or chase it away.” L50: “Block the line”.

10. **L55–56, Naming.** “Absolute pin” is never named, so “relative” has no contrast. Rewrite: “The pin on the board is absolute, because the king is behind the knight. A relative pin has another piece behind, so the pinned piece may move, but the piece behind can then be captured.”

11. **L96–97, reader.** “Back rank” is never defined. Rewrite L96: “Your king on its back rank behind its own pawns, with an enemy rook or queen able to reach that rank.” L97 opening: “A back-rank mate is checkmate by a rook or queen along the back rank, the row where the king started.”

12. **L98, reader.** “Make an escape square with h3.” Rewrite: “Make an escape square with h3, so the king can step to h2.”

13. **L13–14, Unsourced and Overreach.** “again and again in casual chess” has no source, and “a clear way out” clashes with the fork, where the rook is lost (L72). See C1.

14. **L116, ambiguous “it”.** “…and nothing protects it.” Rewrite: “The pawn on d6 attacks the knight on e5, and no white piece protects the knight.”

15. **L79, Unsourced.** “Watch enemy knight jumps”. No source states fork prevention. Accept as advice or replace.
