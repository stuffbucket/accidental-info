# Sources for An Accidental Guide to… Hosting Thanksgiving

All read on 22 October 2026. Excerpts are verbatim and short. Public sources only: the US
government's food safety pages for every safety fact and number, and two culinary
publishers for planning and make-ahead advice.

How the FSIS pages were read: www.fsis.usda.gov and www.foodsafety.gov answer automated
fetches with HTTP 403. Their text was read from the Internet Archive's copies of the same
addresses, made on the dates given under each source (web.archive.org/web/<date>/<address>).
The sources page lists the official addresses. Before publishing, a person should open the
official pages in a browser and confirm the numbers haven't changed.

US Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday 26 November.

## 1. Turkey size, thawing, roasting, the thermometer, resting

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), "Let's Talk Turkey—A Consumer Guide to
Safely Roasting a Turkey".
<https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/lets-talk-turkey-roasting>
Read from the Internet Archive copy of 23 September 2026. Page date: none shown in the
extract.

Turkey size:
- Fresh: "Allow 1 pound of turkey per person."
- Fresh: "Buy your turkey only 1 to 2 days before you plan to cook it."
- Frozen: "Allow 1 pound of turkey per person."
- Frozen pre-stuffed: "DO NOT THAW before cooking. Cook from the frozen state." "Allow 1¼ pounds of turkey per person."

Thawing:
- "There are three ways to thaw your turkey safely — in the refrigerator, in cold water, or in the microwave oven."
- Refrigerator: "In the Refrigerator (40 °F or below) Allow approximately 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds"
- Refrigerator table: "4 to 12 pounds 1 to 3 days", "12 to 16 pounds 3 to 4 days", "16 to 20 pounds 4 to 5 days", "20 to 24 pounds 5 to 6 days"
- "Keep the turkey in its original wrapper. Place it on a tray or in a pan to catch any juices that may leak. A thawed turkey can remain in the refrigerator for 1 to 2 days."
- Cold water: "Allow approximately 30 minutes per pound", table "12 to 16 pounds 6 to 8 hours", "16 to 20 pounds 8 to 10 hours"
- "Submerge your wrapped turkey in cold tap water. Change the water every 30 minutes. Cook the turkey immediately after it is thawed. Do not refreeze."
- "REMINDER: Remove the giblets from the turkey cavities after thawing. Cook separately."

Roasting:
- "Set your oven temperature no lower than 325 °F."
- "Place your turkey or turkey breast on a rack in a shallow roasting pan."
- "For optimum safety, stuffing a turkey is not recommended. For more even cooking, it is recommended you cook your stuffing outside the bird in a casserole."
- "The stuffing must reach a safe minimum internal temperature of 165 °F."
- Timetable, "(325 °F oven temperature)", "These times are approximate. Always use a food thermometer to check the internal temperature of your turkey and stuffing."
- Unstuffed: "8 to 12 pounds 2¾ to 3 hours", "12 to 14 pounds 3 to 3¾ hours", "14 to 18 pounds 3¾ to 4¼ hours", "18 to 20 pounds 4¼ to 4½ hours", "20 to 24 pounds 4½ to 5 hours"
- Frozen: "It is safe to cook a turkey from the frozen state. The cooking time will take at least 50 percent longer than recommended for a fully thawed turkey. Remember to remove the giblet packages during the cooking time. Remove carefully with tongs or a fork."

Thermometer:
- "A whole turkey is safe when cooked to a minimum internal temperature of 165 °F, as measured with a food thermometer. Check the internal temperature in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast."
- "If your turkey has a "pop-up" temperature indicator, it is recommended that you also check the internal temperature of the turkey in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast with a food thermometer."
- "If using an oven-proof food thermometer, place it in the turkey at the start of the cooking cycle. It will allow you to check the internal temperature of the turkey while it is cooking."

Resting:
- "For quality, let the turkey stand for 20 minutes before carving to allow juices to set. The turkey will carve more easily."

## 2. Thanksgiving: buying, washing, stuffing, the two-hour rule, leftovers

FSIS, "Your Safe Thanksgiving Guide".
<https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/your-safe-thanksgiving-guide>
Read from the Internet Archive copy of 23 September 2026.

- Fresh: "The "fresh" label means the turkey has never been chilled below 26 degrees F. Fresh turkeys should not be purchased until one or two days before Thanksgiving"
- Fresh: "If there is no manufacturer’s tag, then purchase a fresh turkey the Tuesday or Wednesday before Thanksgiving at the earliest."
- Leftovers timing: "Leftovers (including appetizers, side dishes and the turkey) should be stored within two hours of cooking (if not being held above 140 degrees F)."
- Frozen: "A frozen turkey is a turkey that has been cooled to 0 degrees F or lower. Most turkeys sold in the United States are frozen. When purchasing a frozen turkey, make sure to leave enough time for it to defrost."
- "Thawing a frozen turkey on the kitchen counter or in hot water is not safe."
- Microwave: "It is not common that a whole turkey will fit in the microwave."
- Washing: "Washing or rinsing raw turkey before you cook your Thanksgiving meal is risky because bacteria can spread around your kitchen and to other foods you may be preparing. To avoid this risk, it is safest to not wash raw turkey."
- Stuffing: "USDA does not recommend stuffing a whole turkey because it increases the risk of cross-contamination and takes longer to cook. For optimal safety and uniform doneness, cook stuffing separately."
- "do not stuff a turkey the night before cooking it."
- Two-hour rule: "Food that is between the temperatures of 40 degrees F and 140 degrees F is in the Danger Zone and only has a limited time (2 hours or 1 hour if above 90 degrees F) before it becomes a food safety risk. Remember to refrigerate food within two hours, and within one hour if it's a hot day (above 90 degrees F)."
- Leftovers: "Dividing leftovers into smaller portions (including carving the turkey) and refrigerating or freezing them in covered shallow containers helps cool leftovers more quickly."
- "Thanksgiving leftovers are safe in the refrigerator for up to four days or until the Monday after Thanksgiving to eat them, or you can place them in the freezer to enjoy later."
- "Regardless of how it is cooked, the internal temperature of cooked turkey must be a minimum of 165 degrees F."
- Reheat: "Before you eat leftovers, reheat them to 165 degrees F."

FSIS, "Turkey Basics: Handling Cooked Dinners".
<https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/turkey-basics-handling-cooked-dinners>
Read from the Internet Archive copy of 23 September 2026. Page date: "Last Updated: Sep 25, 2024".

- "Keeping food warm is not enough. Harmful bacteria multiply fastest between 40 and 140 degrees F."
- "Stuffing and side dishes must also stay hot (at 140 degrees F or above)."
- "Reheat thoroughly to 165 degrees F or until hot and steaming. Bring gravy to a rolling boil."
- "The microwave oven" and "Side dishes can go from the freezer to the oven." (not used)

## 5. Planning, make-ahead, the oven, helpers

Food Network Kitchen, "Thanksgiving Countdown: How to Prepare for Thanksgiving Dinner Like
a Pro". Food Network, 20 October 2021.
<https://www.foodnetwork.com/holidays-and-parties/articles/thanksgiving-countdown-planner>

- Turkey size for leftovers: "Assume 2 pounds per adult and 1 pound per child (to guarantee leftovers)."
- One week out: "Prepare a cooking schedule."
- Oven: "Figure out what can't be cooked along with the turkey in the oven, either in terms of temperature or space."
- Two days out: "Make cranberry sauce and soup." "These dishes can sit in the fridge for 2 days."
- One day out: "Set the table."
- Helpers: "This is the time to ask if your great-aunt makes an awesome stuffing that she'd like to bring."
- Helpers: "Smart tip: put non-cooking household members in charge of cleaning."
- Helpers: "Don't hesitate to press guests into service"

## Worked numbers (arithmetic from the FSIS tables, not new facts)

- A 16- to 20-pound turkey takes 4 to 5 days in the fridge. Five days before Thursday
  26 November 2026 is Saturday 21 November.
- A 14- to 18-pound unstuffed turkey takes 3¾ to 4¼ hours at 325 °F. With 20 minutes of
  resting, dinner at 4 p.m. means the turkey goes in by about 11:15 a.m.
- 165 °F is 74 °C (FoodSafety.gov chart).

## Not stated

- No source here says how much meat a pound of whole turkey gives once the bones are off.
  The guide doesn't say it.
- FSIS doesn't say when to start checking the temperature. The guide says the times are
  approximate and the thermometer decides.

## Where sources disagree

- Turkey size: FSIS says 1 pound per person. Food Network says 2 pounds per adult and 1 per
  child, "to guarantee leftovers". The guide gives FSIS's number for dinner and Food
  Network's for leftovers.
- Resting: FSIS says 20 minutes, for quality. Food Network says 1 hour, tented with foil.
  The guide says at least 20 minutes, which both allow, and uses the time for the sides
  (King Arthur).
- Thawing: Food Network says to start three days out. FSIS's table gives 4 to 6 days for
  a turkey over 16 pounds. The guide uses FSIS.
