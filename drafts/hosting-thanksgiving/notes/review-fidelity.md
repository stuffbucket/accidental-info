## Arithmetic checked (no finding)

- 26 November 2026 is a Thursday, the fourth in November (1 November 2026 is a Sunday, so the Thursdays are 5, 12, 19 and 26). Saturday 21 and Wednesday 25 November are correct.
- Thaw: a 16-pound turkey sits on the boundary of the FSIS rows "12 to 16 pounds 3 to 4 days" and "16 to 20 pounds 4 to 5 days". The 24-hours-per-4-to-5-pounds rule gives 3.2 to 4 days. Saturday 21 to Thursday morning is about 5 days, so the bird is thawed by Tuesday or Wednesday and waits 1 to 2 days, which FSIS allows ("A thawed turkey can remain in the refrigerator for 1 to 2 days"). Line 73 uses the 16-to-20 row, the safer of the two. Correct.
- Roast: 16 pounds falls in "14 to 18 pounds 3¾ to 4¼ hours" at 325 °F. 11:15 a.m. plus 3¾ hours is 3:00 p.m. Plus 4¼ hours is 3:30 p.m. From 3:30 p.m., 20 minutes of rest ends at 3:50 p.m., before the 4 p.m. carve. If the bird is done at 3:00 p.m., it rests an hour, which Food Network allows. Correct.
- Leftovers: Thursday plus four days is Monday 30 November, which matches "until the Monday after Thanksgiving". Correct.
- 165 °F is 74 °C, as the FoodSafety.gov chart gives. Correct.
- Wednesday tasks match the planning sources: pies (King Arthur, "Wednesday is pie-baking day"), stuffing chilled in its dish (King Arthur), cranberry sauce (Food Network, "Two days out", and "These dishes can sit in the fridge for 2 days"), set the table (Food Network, "One day out").

## Overreach

### O1. Two-hour clock starts at the wrong time (line 41, "How does the week fit together?")

**Guide:** "> | Within two hours | Leftovers into the fridge, in shallow covered containers. |"

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Your Safe Thanksgiving Guide, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/your-safe-thanksgiving-guide): "Food that is between the temperatures of 40 degrees F and 140 degrees F is in the Danger Zone and only has a limited time (2 hours or 1 hour if above 90 degrees F) before it becomes a food safety risk."

**Verdict:** overreach. Placed after "4 p.m. Carve and eat", the row reads as two hours from 4 p.m. FSIS counts the time food spends below 140 °F, and the turkey starts cooling when it leaves the oven at 3:30 p.m. or earlier. The row gives the reader up to half an hour more than the source does.

**Corrected:** "> | By 5:30 p.m. | Leftovers into the fridge, in shallow covered containers. Cooked food can sit out for two hours at most. |"

### O2. Fresh turkey "needs no thawing" (line 84, entry 02 Thawing)

**Guide:** "A fresh turkey has never been chilled below 26 °F, so it needs no thawing."

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Your Safe Thanksgiving Guide, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/your-safe-thanksgiving-guide): "The "fresh" label means the turkey has never been chilled below 26 degrees F." and "A frozen turkey is a turkey that has been cooled to 0 degrees F or lower."

**Verdict:** overreach. FSIS defines fresh by the 26 °F floor. It doesn’t say a fresh turkey needs no thawing, and 26 °F is below freezing. The contrast with frozen is sourced. The "so" isn’t.

**Corrected:** "A fresh turkey has never been chilled below 26 °F. A frozen one has been cooled to 0 °F or lower. Buy a fresh turkey only one or two days before cooking."

### O3. The turkey as "the one dish with firm numbers" (lines 17 to 18, "What does hosting Thanksgiving take?")

**Guide:** "Most of the dinner forgives a late start or ten extra minutes. The turkey is the one dish with firm numbers: how big, how long to thaw, how long to roast and how hot inside."

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Let’s Talk Turkey, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/lets-talk-turkey-roasting): "The stuffing must reach a safe minimum internal temperature of 165 °F." Also FSIS, Your Safe Thanksgiving Guide: "Remember to refrigerate food within two hours".

**Verdict:** overreach. The guide itself gives firm numbers for the stuffing (165 °F, line 151) and for all food left out (two hours, line 153). No source says the rest of the dinner forgives ten extra minutes.

**Corrected:** "Most side dishes forgive a late start. The turkey has the firmest numbers: how big, how long to thaw, how long to roast and how hot inside."

### O4. Washing "spreads" bacteria (line 152, Common questions)

**Guide:** "> | Should I wash the turkey? | No. Washing raw turkey spreads bacteria around the kitchen. |"

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Your Safe Thanksgiving Guide, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/your-safe-thanksgiving-guide): "Washing or rinsing raw turkey before you cook your Thanksgiving meal is risky because bacteria can spread around your kitchen and to other foods you may be preparing."

**Verdict:** overreach. FSIS says bacteria can spread. The guide says washing does spread them.

**Corrected:** "> | Should I wash the turkey? | No. Washing raw turkey can spread bacteria around the kitchen and onto other food. |"

### O5. Cold-water time stated as exact (line 150, Common questions)

**Guide:** "Thaw it in its wrapper in cold water, 30 minutes per pound, changing the water every 30 minutes."

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Let’s Talk Turkey, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/lets-talk-turkey-roasting): "Allow approximately 30 minutes per pound"

**Verdict:** overreach, minor. FSIS gives the rate as approximate. Line 72 has the same slip for the fridge rate ("Allow approximately 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds"), though the range there already signals an estimate.

**Corrected:** "Thaw it in its wrapper in cold water, about 30 minutes per pound, changing the water every 30 minutes."

## Unsourced

**Guide:** "> | Two weeks before | Buy the turkey, about 1 pound for each guest. Buy a fresh one only a day or two before. |"

The thaw day in the next row, Saturday 21 November, already sets the latest buying day, so the row can use that date instead. ## Naming

### N1. "Pop-up timer" (lines 122 to 125, entry 04 Thermometer)

**Guide:** "## Confused with: Pop-up timer" and "Some turkeys come with a pop-up timer. Check those three places with a food thermometer as well, even after it pops."

**Source** (USDA FSIS, Let’s Talk Turkey, https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/poultry/lets-talk-turkey-roasting): "If your turkey has a "pop-up" temperature indicator, it is recommended that you also check the internal temperature of the turkey in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast with a food thermometer."

**Verdict:** naming. FSIS calls it a pop-up temperature indicator. "Timer" suggests it measures time, which is the confusion the entry should clear up. "Pop-up timer" is the common shop name, so keep it but give the FSIS name once.

**Corrected:** "Some turkeys come with a pop-up timer, a temperature indicator that pops out. Check those three places with a food thermometer as well, even after it pops."
