# Hosting Thanksgiving: reader panel

Reviewed: `drafts/hosting-thanksgiving/guide.md`, version 0.3.0, and its 7-page PDF (page
images and `pdftotext` output in `/tmp/ag-pages/ht`). Each page was read as three readers
with no access to the sources:

- **First-time host.** Has never roasted a turkey, is nervous, and needs to get dinner on
  the table on Thursday 26 November without making anyone ill.
- **Experienced cook.** Hosts every year, checks every number, and has no patience for fluff
  or errors.
- **Busy relative.** Skims the PDF on a phone the night before and reads only what looks
  urgent.

The voice pass checks the guide against the review skill's `checklist.md`.

The guide is at its five-minute word limit. Every rewrite below is counted against the line
it replaces, and the full set saves 5 words (see "Word budget" at the end). Rewrites marked
"check sources" add a fact that the fidelity review must confirm before use.

## Top ten fixes

Most serious first.

**1. The 3:30 p.m. row gives a fixed time for a step that depends on the thermometer.**
Page 3, line 39: "Thursday, 3:30 p.m. | Once the thermometer reads 165 °F, take the turkey
out to rest. Bake the stuffing and rolls." The turkey went in at 11:15 for 3¾ to 4¼ hours,
so it can be done at 3 p.m. The first-time host doesn't know when to start checking and
may wait until 3:30. The busy relative reads "3:30, take the turkey out" and skips the
thermometer. The experienced cook does the sum and sees that 3:30 is the latest finish, not
the time. "Rest" also has no length.
Rewrite line 39: "Thursday, from 3 p.m. | Check the thermometer. At 165 °F, take the turkey
out to rest until 4 p.m. Bake the stuffing, then the rolls."

**2. Only a 16-pound turkey gets a roasting time, and the timetable the guide names is never shown.**
Page 5, lines 104–105: "A roasting timetable gives the turkey’s hours by weight at 325 °F.
An oven plan fits every other dish around those hours." Line 18 promises firm numbers for
"how long to roast". The first-time host with eight guests buys an 8-pound turkey and has
no time to plan with. The experienced cook asks where the timetable is.
Rewrite lines 104–105 (check sources against the USDA timetable for unstuffed turkeys):
"At 325 °F, a turkey of 8 to 12 pounds takes 2¾ to 3 hours, and 12 to 14 pounds 3 to 3¾.
An oven plan fits other dishes around those hours."

**3. Nothing tells the host to check the thaw before Thursday morning.**
Page 3, line 37, and page 4, line 71. The first time a frozen middle shows up is Thursday
at 11 a.m. A 16-pound turkey then needs about 8 hours in cold water (line 150), so dinner
is late. The first-time host also has no way to tell a turkey is thawed: line 71 says
"Identify by | A frozen turkey and a date on the calendar."
Rewrite line 37: "Bake the pies, make and chill the cranberry sauce and stuffing, and set
the table. Feel inside the turkey for ice."
Rewrite line 71: "A thawed turkey has no ice inside."

**4. "Within two hours" has no starting point.**
Page 3, line 41: "Within two hours | Leftovers into the fridge, in shallow covered
containers." Two hours from what? The busy relative reads it as two hours after dinner
ends. Every other Thursday row is a clock time.
Rewrite line 41 label: "Thursday, by 5 p.m." (two hours after the earliest finish; check
sources for the starting point).

**5. The first row says to buy the turkey two weeks early, then says not to.**
Page 3, line 35: "Two weeks before | Buy the turkey, about 1 pound for each guest. Buy a
fresh one only a day or two before." The busy relative stops at "Two weeks before": before
what? The row is also the only one without a date.
Rewrite line 35: "By 12 November | Buy a frozen turkey, about 1 pound for each guest. Buy a
fresh one on 24 or 25 November."

**6. The thermometer page warns about stuffing but not about the bone, and "innermost thigh" is unclear.**
Page 5, line 120: "Watch for | Stuffing inside the turkey". The reason only comes on page 6,
so the first-time host doesn't see what stuffing has to do with a thermometer. The common
error with a thermometer is a probe resting on bone. Line 113, "Check the innermost thigh
and wing", reads as if there were an inner and outer thigh. "The thermometer decides" is
personification.
Rewrite line 120: "The probe touching bone" (check sources).
Rewrite line 113: "Roasting times are estimates, so go by the thermometer. Check deep in the
thigh, the wing and the thickest part of the breast."

**7. The Helpers page has a garden-path sentence and an off-topic warning.**
Page 6, line 133: "Give the people who don’t cook the cleaning." All three readers first
parse "the people who don’t cook the cleaning". Line 140, "Watch for | Food left out over
two hours", is a food-safety rule, not a helper problem. The real helper problem is a
guest who arrives with a dish that needs your oven.
Rewrite line 133: "Guests who don’t cook can wash up."
Rewrite line 140: "Dishes that need your oven"

**8. Nobody tells the first-time host to take the neck and giblets out.**
Page 3, line 38: "Turkey into a 325 °F oven, for about 3¾ to 4¼ hours." This is the
classic first-time mistake, and the first-time host doesn't know the bag is in there.
Rewrite line 38 (check sources): "Remove the neck and giblets. Turkey into a 325 °F oven
for 3¾ to 4¼ hours."

**9. The field-mark labels and "Watch for" answers don't read on their own.**
Pages 4–6. "Field marks" and "Identify by" mean nothing to the busy relative, who skips
the grey boxes. "Watch for" can mean "look out for" or "look for". Line 80, "Watch for |
The counter or hot water", can be read as advice to use them. The label fix is in the
builder (see the format leakage report). The answer can be fixed now.
Rewrite line 80: "Counter or hot-water thawing"

**10. The Helpers example contradicts the countdown, and the Cooks contrast is filler.**
Page 6, line 132: "One guest brings the pie". Line 37 has the host baking the pies on
Wednesday. Lines 142–144, "Confused with: Cooks / Cooks make food. Helpers can also set the
table, wash up or watch the clock.", repeats line 132 and no one confuses helpers with
cooks. The useful contrast is a potluck, where guests choose the dish.
Rewrite line 132: "One guest brings a salad, and another clears the table."
Rewrite line 142: "## Confused with: Potluck"
Rewrite line 144: "At a potluck, guests choose what to bring. Helpers get the dish or job
you choose."

## Still missing for the first-time host

These gaps remain even after the rewrites. Each would need cuts somewhere else before it
could go in.

- How to set up the turkey: breast up, in a shallow roasting pan, oven preheated.
- Stovetop dishes (potatoes, gravy) and when to make them. The countdown covers only
  oven dishes.
- What thermometer to buy. "A food thermometer with a metal probe" (line 111) doesn't
  say whether it should be instant-read or stay in the oven.

## Verdict

The first-time host would finish the guide and buy and thaw the turkey in time. On
Thursday they would follow the 3:30 time over the thermometer, and they would have no
roasting time for a smaller bird. Fixes 1 to 3 close that gap. The experienced cook
finds the numbers sound and the arithmetic right (16 pounds, Saturday thaw, 11:15 start).
They would doubt the 3:30 row, the stuffing timing and the pie contradiction. The busy
relative gets what they need from page 3 once its labels are clock times with no
contradictions, and would skip pages 4 to 6 as they stand.
