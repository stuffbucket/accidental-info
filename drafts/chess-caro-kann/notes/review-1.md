# Review 1: The Caro-Kann for Black

Boards: I checked all six positions square by square against their captions, viewed from Black’s side, and they match. Arrows, highlights and side to move are right.

## 1. Most serious

**1. L157, L163, trap 1.** The guide tells the reader to play 4…Bf5 (L136, L144), so “After 4…Nd7” comes from nowhere. On the board, the pawn on e7 seems able to take d6. The real reason it can’t is the queen on e2, which the text never mentions.
L157: “If you play 4…Nd7 instead of 4…Bf5, answer 5.Qe2 with 5…Ndf6. After 5…Ngf6, 6.Nd6 is checkmate.”
L163: “The knight on d6 gives checkmate. Your own pieces block the king, and the pawn on e7 can’t take the knight because White’s queen on e2 would then attack your king.”

**2. L42, Overreach.** “The same plan works after each choice”. No source says this. In the Advance, the plan’s order (L64, L91: “Bishop out, then …e6”) walks into 4.h4 e6?? 5.g4, which Wikibooks says loses the bishop.
L41–43: “…or defend it with a knight in the Main line, where you take the pawn on e4 anyway. Against each choice, bring the light bishop out, play …e6, develop your other pieces and castle.”

**3. L167, Wrong, kicker.** “When White’s pawns run at your bishop, give the bishop a square to retreat to.” This fits …h6 but not 4…h5, which gives no new square. It is also an aphorism ending.
“When White pushes the h-pawn toward your bishop, answer with your own h-pawn before …e6. Play …h6 in the Main line and …h5 in the Advance.”

**4. L13 on, reader.** The notation (1.e4, …d5, Nxe4, #) is used from the first line. The only pointer comes at L18 and sends the reader to another guide.
L17–18: “The Caro-Kann answers 1.e4 only. Against 1.d4, a simple start is 1…d5. In the moves, K, Q, R, B and N name the pieces, a bare square is a pawn move, x is a capture and … marks a Black move.”

**5. L43, L57, L110, reader.** “develop” is never explained.
Add at the end of L43: “Developing a piece means moving it off its starting square.”

**6. L137, Wrong (besides h7).** “White often replies 5.Ng3 Bg6” gives Black’s move to White. Trap row L158 already covers h4.
“White often replies 5.Ng3, and the bishop drops back to g6.”

**7. L83, Wrong.** “past your pawn”. L82 and the board show the pawn beside d5.
“The Advance is White pushing the e-pawn to e5 with 3.e5.”

**8. L84, reader.** “pawn chain” is not defined.
“After …e6, your bishop on f8 supports …c5, which attacks White’s pawn on d4.”

**9. L148–149, Overreach.** The source says “Usually, both moves reach the same position”. The text also implies that only 3.Nc3 defends e4.
“Both 3.Nc3 and 3.Nd2 defend e4. After 3…dxe4 4.Nxe4 they usually reach the same position.”

**10. L14–15, Unsourced, MP.** “you can play it with a short plan instead of memorizing long lines” has no source. “It is known…” is pronoun-led.
“The Caro-Kann is known for being solid.”

**11. Exchange, reader gap.** Wikipedia gives 7.Qb3 as White’s usual next move. The queen on b3 then hits b7 and d5, and the guide gives no answer. The Panov gets no answer either. Source a reply, or tell the reader plainly that these lines need more study.

## 2. Naming and lesser fidelity

**L13, Overreach.** The sources define the opening by 1.e4 c6 alone.
“…that begins 1.e4 c6, usually followed by 2.d4 d5.”

**L25, Naming.** The sources say “light-squared”, and nothing tells the reader why it is “light”.
“Black’s bishop that starts on c8 and moves on the light squares.”

**L28, Naming, reader.** Wikipedia uses “main line” for 2.d4 d5, and Chess.com calls 4…Bf5 the Classical. Also, “then recaptures there” skips Black’s capture.
“White defends e4 with a knight. You take the pawn, and the knight takes back on e4.”

**L119, Naming.** The source has “Panov–Botvinnik Attack”. The sources-page title at L177 also differs from sources.md.

## 3. Format leakage and prose

**L122, SN.** “and it isn’t covered here”.
“The Panov Attack starts with 4.c4 instead, and White’s c-pawn then attacks your pawn on d5.”

**L49, L55, vague.** “where it can take part” and “outside the pawns”.
L49: “The bishop reached f5 before …e6, so the pawns on c6, d5 and e6 don’t block it.”
L55: “Black’s bishop from c8 on f5 or g4, in front of the pawns on c6, d5 and e6.”

I found no rhetorical questions, unnamed voices or borrowed framing.
