# Is there a right way<br/>to load a dishwasher? {#why}

There is a right way to load a dishwasher, and the people who make them mostly agree on it, which is more than can be said for some kitchens. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a dish comes clean when its dirty side faces the spray and nothing blocks the way.

The makers disagree on a few details, such as cutlery and glasses. When they do, your own dishwasher’s manual wins.

> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | Bottom rack | The lower rack, for plates, pots, pans and big items. |
> | Top rack | The upper rack, for cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. |
> | Cutlery basket | The basket that holds knives, forks and spoons upright. |
> | Spray arm | The turning arm under a rack that sprays water up. |
> | Detergent | What goes in the dispenser and breaks down food. |

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away. Before you start, check that nothing blocks the detergent dispenser or stops a spray arm turning.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off large bits of food, but don’t rinse dishes under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates on their sides between the tines, facing the middle, and put pots and pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. Keep platters clear of the spray arm. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | Watch for | Blocking the dispenser |
> | Cannot | Clean stacked dishes |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Makers differ on glasses in the bottom rack. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | Watch for | Glasses touching |
> | Cannot | Save unsafe plastic |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, and most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer for knives and forks, and handles down cleans better. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | Watch for | Spoons nesting |
> | Cannot | End the argument |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing. Many US manuals use that name, so look for either in yours.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays wash water up through it. Each arm needs room to turn. |
| So what | A pan handle or utensil hanging through a rack can stop an arm. Turn each spray arm by hand before you start, to check that it spins. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | Watch for | Tall or hanging items |
> | Cannot | Reach blocked surfaces |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves water around the dishwasher. The filter catches food from that water and needs cleaning regularly.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet or pod is enough. |
| So what | Put it in the dispenser and close the lid. Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | Watch for | Hand dish soap |
> | Cannot | Escape a blocked lid |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food during washing. Rinse aid helps water drain off, which improves drying and reduces spots.

# The usual arguments {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Rinse first? | No. Scrape off food and skip the tap. If dishes will sit overnight, run the dishwasher’s rinse cycle instead. |
> | Knives, forks and spoons up or down? | Most makers say knives point down. Mix the rest so nothing nests. Makers split on forks and spoons. |
> | Pod in the dispenser or loose in the tub? | In the dispenser, with the lid closed. |
> | Run it only when full? | When you can, since a dishwasher uses about the same water and energy either way. Leave room for the water to reach everything. |
> | Run the hot tap first? | Whirlpool says yes, because cool water can mean a poor wash. Run the nearest hot tap until the water is hot. |

When advice differs, your dishwasher’s manual wins.
