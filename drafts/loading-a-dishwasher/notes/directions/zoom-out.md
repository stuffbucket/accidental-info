# How much does it matter<br/>how you load a dishwasher? {#why}

Up close, a lot. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a bowl facing the spray comes clean and a spoon tucked inside another spoon stays dirty.

From further away, less. A kitchen holds more than a dishwasher, an evening holds more than a load, and a year holds a great many evenings. Each step back makes the spoon a little smaller. Where makers disagree, such as on forks, your own manual wins, and you can take the step back.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Up close, put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away, and check that nothing blocks the detergent dispenser or stops a spray arm turning. That takes a few minutes. From the kitchen table, it looks like someone putting plates in a box.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off large bits of food, but don’t rinse dishes under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates between the tines facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. Keep platters clear of the spray arm. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Up close | Plates facing the middle |
> | From the table | Somebody humming |
> | In a year | The same plates, again and again |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Makers differ on glasses in the bottom rack. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Up close | Glasses not touching |
> | From the table | Some clinking |
> | In a year | Who always gets the good mug |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, and most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, Bosch’s two manuals disagree, and GE’s two help pages disagree too. At the scale of the basket it looks like a long war with a maker on every side. From the table it’s a small basket of forks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Up close | Knives point down |
> | From the table | Whether anyone sighed |
> | In a year | Nobody can say which way |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing. Many US manuals use that name, so look for either in yours.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays wash water up through it. Turn each arm by hand before you start, to check that it spins. |
| So what | A pan handle hanging through a rack can stop an arm, and that rack comes out dirty. One dirty cup goes in the next load, which is tomorrow. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Up close | Nothing hanging down |
> | From the table | Whether anyone hovered |
> | In a year | A handful of cups washed twice |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves water around the dishwasher. The filter catches food from that water and needs cleaning regularly.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet or pod is enough. |
| So what | Put it in the dispenser and close the lid. Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Up close | One tablet, lid closed |
> | From the table | A quiet hum starting |
> | In a year | One good story about hand soap |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food during washing. Rinse aid helps water drain off, which improves drying and reduces spots.

# The usual arguments,<br/>from further away {#apart}

> | Question | Up close | From further away |
> |---|---|---|
> | Rinse first? | No. Scrape off food and skip the tap. | Nobody at the table can tell who rinsed. |
> | Forks up or down? | Makers split, so your manual decides. | Ask why they load them that way. A maker probably agrees with them. |
> | Fix it after them? | Only if a spray arm can’t turn. | The person who loaded it can always tell. |
> | Run it only when full? | When you can, since it uses about the same water and energy either way. | A few half loads won’t be remembered. |
> | Run the hot tap first? | Whirlpool says yes, until the water is hot. | A minute leaning on the counter, talking. |

Further out still, ENERGY STAR says a new certified dishwasher uses less than half the energy of washing by hand and saves 8,400 gallons of water a year, whichever way the forks face.
