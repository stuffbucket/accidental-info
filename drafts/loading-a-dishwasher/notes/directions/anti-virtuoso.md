# Do you have to be good<br/>at loading a dishwasher? {#why}

No, since the dishwasher does most of the work. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack and detergent breaks down the food, so your part is short: scrape the plates, face the dirty sides toward the spray, leave room and close the lid on one tablet.

Past that, there’s no perfect load to aim for. Makers disagree on forks, spoons, glasses, big plates and wood, and Bosch’s two manuals disagree with each other. A perfect load gets the same applause as any other load, which is none.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away, and check that nothing blocks the detergent dispenser or stops a spray arm turning. That covers most of it. Then close the door, let out a breath and go and sit with whoever is still at the table.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off large bits of food, but don’t rinse dishes under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food. Rinsed plates leave the detergent with nothing to do.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates between the tines facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. GE and Frigidaire put large items along the edge and Miele puts very large plates in the middle, so wherever someone else put the big plate, a maker agrees with them. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | Get right | Room for the spray arm |
> | Leave alone | Someone else’s big plate |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Glasses can be damaged if they touch, so give them room. Mugs can go in any order and still come out as mugs. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | Get right | Glasses apart |
> | Leave alone | The order of the mugs |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, and most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, which gives you both answers from one maker. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | Get right | Knives point down |
> | Leave alone | Forks and spoons |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing. Many US manuals use that name, so look for either in yours.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays wash water up through it. Each arm needs room to turn. |
| So what | A pan handle hanging through a rack can stop an arm. Turn each arm by hand before you start, and if it spins, the load is good enough to run. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | Get right | A free spin |
> | Leave alone | The door, once it’s shut |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves water around the dishwasher. The filter catches food from that water and needs cleaning now and then.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet or pod is enough. |
| So what | Put it in the dispenser and close the lid. Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. Every maker that mentions it says the same, which makes it rare. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | Get right | Dishwasher detergent only |
> | Leave alone | A second tablet |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food during washing. Rinse aid helps water drain off, which improves drying and reduces spots.

# The usual arguments {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Rinse first? | No. Scrape off food and skip the tap. If dishes will sit overnight, use the rinse cycle. |
> | Should I fix the load after them? | Only if a spray arm can’t turn. Otherwise close the door. A fork the wrong way round still gets washed. |
> | Is it better to do it alone? | No. Load it with whoever is in the kitchen. Hand over the plates, take the cutlery and talk about something else. |
> | Does it need concentration? | Less than it looks. Keep the face you had at dinner. A frown at the cutlery basket makes people think the dishwasher is broken. |
> | Run the hot tap first? | Whirlpool says yes. Run the nearest hot tap until the water is hot, and lean on the counter while you wait. |

When advice differs, your dishwasher’s manual wins. Read it before you bring anything up, since it may be on their side.
