# Which of you is loading<br/>the dishwasher right? {#why}

In one kitchen, one person loads the cutlery handles up and the other loads it handles down. Call them Handles Up and Handles Down. Each is sure, each has a dishwasher maker on their side, and the argument is years old and going well.

They agree on the basics. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a dish comes clean when its dirty side faces the spray and nothing blocks the way. Where they disagree, the makers disagree too, which both of them think is the best part.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Both put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away, and both check that nothing blocks the detergent dispenser or a spray arm. Neither has mentioned this, since there’s nothing in it to argue about.

> [!TIP]
> Both scrape off food and neither rinses. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food. This is the one argument they’ve never had, and they’re a little sorry about it.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower rack, with the strongest spray. |
| What | Handles Up puts big platters along the side. GE and Frigidaire say large items go along the edge, where they don’t block the spray arm. |
| So what | Handles Down puts very large plates in the middle. Miele says that’s where they go, and Handles Down mentions Miele whenever a platter comes out. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Handles Up | Big things at the edge |
> | Handles Down | Biggest plate in the middle |
> | Both | Plates face the middle, pans face down |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for cups, small bowls and plastics. |
| What | Handles Up keeps every glass on top. GE says never put glasses in the lower rack, and Frigidaire says upper rack only. |
| So what | Handles Down sometimes puts a tall glass at the bottom. Miele says glasses can go in the lower basket too. Handles Up asked where it said so, read it, and said “oh, that’s good”. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Handles Up | Glasses on top only |
> | Handles Down | Glasses where they fit |
> | Both | Plastics on top, where it’s cooler |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind, which would end the argument, so neither of them wants one.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | Handles Up loads knives and forks handles up, to protect hands. Miele, GE and Bosch say that’s safer. |
| So what | Handles Down loads forks and spoons handles down, so they come out cleaner. Miele says that too, in the next sentence, and Bosch’s two manuals take one side each, which both count as a win. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Handles Up | Safer for hands |
> | Handles Down | Cleaner and drier |
> | Both | Knives point down, nothing nests |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing, under its US name.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | Handles Up turns each spray arm by hand before starting, as Miele and Frigidaire suggest. |
| So what | Handles Down looks under each rack for anything hanging through. Each thinks the other’s method is slower, and both catch the same pan handle. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Handles Up | Spin it by hand |
> | Handles Down | Look underneath |
> | Both | An arm that turns |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves water around. The filter catches food from it. Bosch says check it after each wash and Miele says every 50 cycles. Neither of them has a view, which makes it the dullest part of the dishwasher.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Handles Up uses tablets. Whirlpool recommends tablets and packs. |
| So what | Handles Down uses powder for short cycles. Bosch says tablets sometimes don’t dissolve fully on shorter cycles. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Handles Up | Tablets |
> | Handles Down | Powder for short cycles |
> | Both | Never hand dish soap |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food. Rinse aid helps water drain off, so dishes dry with fewer spots.

# How the argument goes {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Who reloads it? | Nobody. Each loads it their own way, and a fork the other way round still gets washed. |
> | How does a round start? | Leaning on the counter while the hot tap runs, as Whirlpool recommends. One of them holds up a fork and raises an eyebrow. |
> | Then what? | The other gives their reason, which is usually better than expected. Handles Up enjoys Handles Down’s reasons, even the ones that lose. |
> | What happens when one of them is away? | The forks all go one way and the kitchen is quieter than either of them likes. |

When advice differs, the manual in the drawer wins. Handles Up has lost two arguments to it and won one, and smiled about all three.
