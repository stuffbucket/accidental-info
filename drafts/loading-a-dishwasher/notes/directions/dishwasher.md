# Is there a right way<br/>to load a dishwasher? {#why}

I’m the dishwasher, so I hear this question more than anyone. I have two spray arms, a filter, a detergent dispenser and no opinion about spoons.

Water sprays up from an arm under each of my racks, so I clean whatever faces the spray and miss whatever is hidden. Past that, the companies that make dishwashers disagree on several details, and I wash what I’m given. When they disagree, my manual wins. It’s in the drawer with the takeaway menus.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where my water can reach its dirty side and drain away. Check that nothing blocks my detergent dispenser or stops a spray arm turning. Then close the door. I can’t start with it open, however good the conversation about the forks is.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off large bits of food, but don’t rinse dishes under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food. When the plates arrive spotless, the enzymes have nothing to do.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | My lower rack, the big one. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates between the tines facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets my strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go there. Keep platters clear of the spray arm, since I can’t move them out of the way myself. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | I notice | A platter in the arm’s way |
> | I don’t notice | Who loaded it |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets my strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | My upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Makers differ on whether glasses can go in the bottom rack, so I have no view on it either. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | I notice | Glasses touching |
> | I don’t notice | The order of the mugs |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in my bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, since my water can’t get between two spoons stuck together. Most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, and Bosch’s two manuals disagree. I’ve washed forks both ways, and from in here they all look like forks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | I notice | Spoons nesting |
> | I don’t notice | Whose idea it was |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing. Many US manuals use that name, so look for either in mine.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each of my racks. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays wash water up through it. Each arm needs room to turn. |
| So what | A pan handle hanging through a rack can stop an arm, and then that rack doesn’t get washed. Turn each arm by hand before you start, to check that it spins. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | I notice | Anything hanging down |
> | I don’t notice | Sighing |

## Confused with: Filter

My spray arms move the water around. My filter catches food from it. Bosch says to check a filter after each wash, and Miele’s machines ask every 50 cycles. I’d settle for now and then.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod or tablet in my door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet is enough. |
| So what | Put it in my dispenser and close the lid. Hand dish soap foams too much, and Bosch says in writing that it can damage machines like me. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | I notice | Hand soap, at once |
> | I don’t notice | Who bought it |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food during washing. Rinse aid helps water run off at the end, so glasses dry with fewer spots. Then the door opens and I let out a long breath of steam, which is the best part of my evening.

# What I hear from in here {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Rinse first? | No. Scrape and skip the tap. If the dishes will sit overnight, use my rinse cycle, which uses a fraction of the water the tap does. |
> | Knives, forks and spoons up or down? | Knives point down. Forks and spoons, I’ve had both and I wash both. |
> | Should you redo it after them? | I’ll wash it either way. What changes is that my door stays open and the kitchen goes quiet. |
> | Only run it when full? | When you can. I use about the same water and energy either way. |
> | Hot tap first? | Whirlpool says yes. Run the nearest hot tap until the water is hot. People lean on the counter and talk while it runs, and I like that part. |

When advice differs, my manual wins. It’s in the drawer, and it may be on their side.
