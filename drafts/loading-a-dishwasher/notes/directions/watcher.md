# Is there a right way<br/>to load a dishwasher? {#why}

I’m at the kitchen table and you’re loading the dishwasher. From here I can see both racks, the cutlery basket and most of your face, which is the best seat in the house when you get to the forks.

You load it the way the makers say to, mostly. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so you face each dirty side toward the spray and leave room. Where the makers disagree, such as on forks, so do we, cheerfully and at length.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

You put each thing where water can reach its dirty side, and check that nothing blocks the dispenser. Then you hand me the colander, because you like seeing where I’ll put it.

> [!TIP]
> You scrape plates and don’t rinse them, since most detergents use enzymes to break down food. I still rinse the tomato pan if it’ll sit, which Frigidaire allows, and you still raise an eyebrow.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. You stand plates facing the middle and pans open side down. When I put a plate in backwards, you give it the closed-mouth smile people give a dog in a hat. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go there. You put the big platter at the edge, as GE says, and I put it in the middle, as Miele says. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | From the table | Plates facing the middle |
> | Still arguing about | The big platter |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray. The top rack is for small, light things, including my mug.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller things. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. You put them open side down, angled and apart, humming as you go. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Makers differ on glasses in the bottom rack. When I brought it up you raised your eyebrows, went to look, came back saying “oh, that’s good”, and put the glass on top anyway. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | From the table | Humming |
> | Still arguing about | One tall glass |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack is a cutlery tray on some machines. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind, and we can’t agree on the kinds.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. You mix them so spoons don’t nest, and knives point down, as most makers say. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, and Bosch’s two manuals disagree. If a maker ever settled it, we’d have to find something else. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | From the table | Knives point down |
> | Still arguing about | Every fork |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing, under its US name.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays water up through it. You spin each arm by hand, close the door, breathe out and come and sit down. |
| So what | A pan handle hanging through a rack can stop an arm, and that rack doesn’t get washed. The ladle comes up most weeks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water up |
> | From the table | A quick spin by hand |
> | Still arguing about | The ladle |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves the water and the filter catches food from it. Bosch says check it after each wash, and Miele every 50 cycles, which is closer to how often you remember.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod or tablet in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. You use one tablet, in the dispenser, lid closed. |
| So what | Hand dish soap foams too much, and Bosch says it can damage the dishwasher. The one time I used it, you laughed first and explained second, and you bring it up whenever you’re losing about the forks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | From the table | One tablet, lid shut |
> | Still arguing about | Nothing, sadly |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food. Rinse aid helps water run off, so glasses dry with fewer spots.

# When I load it {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Do you fix it after me? | No. You leave my forks alone and say they look lonely. |
> | What if you think I’m wrong? | You lean back on the counter, grin, and ask why. Sometimes my reason is better, and you look delighted. |
> | What about when you’re away? | I load the forks your way, to see what it’s like. The kitchen is too quiet. |

When advice differs, the manual in the drawer wins. You’ve been wrong about the spoons once, and you were the one who told me.
