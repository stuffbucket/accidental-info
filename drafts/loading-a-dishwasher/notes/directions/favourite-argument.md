# Which way up<br/>do the forks go? {#why}

It depends which maker you ask, and they don’t agree. That makes the forks one of the best arguments a kitchen can have. It comes round every evening, both sides have a maker behind them, and nothing is at stake but a fork.

The rest of the dishwasher is easier. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a dish comes clean when its dirty side faces the spray and nothing blocks the way. Get that right and save your energy for the forks.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away, and check that nothing blocks the dispenser or a spray arm. Nobody argues about this part, which is why nobody remembers doing it.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off food, but don’t rinse under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most detergents use enzymes to break down food. The makers agree on this, so there’s no fun to be had here.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates between the tines facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. GE puts platters along the sides and Miele puts very large plates in the middle, a good warm-up for the forks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | Agreed | Pans face down |
> | Open for argument | The big plate |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray. The top rack is for small, light and delicate things.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. GE says never put glasses in the bottom rack and Miele says you can, which is handy when the forks go quiet. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | Agreed | Plastics on top |
> | Open for argument | One tall glass |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind, which starts a whole new argument.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack, where the good argument lives. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Knives point down, which most makers agree on, and mixing the pieces stops spoons nesting. |
| So what | Forks and spoons are where it gets good. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, Bosch’s two manuals take one side each, and GE’s two help pages disagree with each other. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | Agreed | Knives point down |
> | Open for argument | Everything else, for ever |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing, under its US name. Nobody has ever argued about which name is right, which seems a waste.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays water up through it, so it needs room to turn. |
| So what | A pan handle or utensil hanging through a rack can stop an arm. Turn each arm by hand before you start. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | Agreed | The arm has to turn |
> | Open for argument | Whose ladle stopped it |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves the water. The filter catches food from it and needs cleaning regularly.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet is enough, in the dispenser with the lid closed. |
| So what | Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. Whirlpool prefers tablets and Bosch suggests powder for short cycles, which will do on a slow night. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | Agreed | Never hand dish soap |
> | Open for argument | Tablet or powder |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food. Rinse aid helps water drain off, so dishes dry with fewer spots.

# Keeping the fork<br/>argument going {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Should someone settle it? | Please don’t. Your manual will pick a side, so read it together and enjoy finding out who’s wrong. |
> | What if the manual sides with them? | Say “oh, that’s good” and ask what else it says. A point lost to a manual makes a good story. |
> | What if they keep loading it their way? | Of course they will. Load it yours on your nights, and both ways get the forks washed. |
> | Is teasing allowed? | Kindly, and in both directions. “Lovely forks” lands better than a sigh. |
> | Where do you hear their side? | Leaning on the counter while the hot tap runs, as Whirlpool advises. Ask why, as if the answer might be good. The answer often is. |

When advice differs, your dishwasher’s manual wins, except on the forks, where it only joins in.
