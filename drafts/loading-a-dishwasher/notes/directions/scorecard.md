# Who’s winning<br/>the dishwasher? {#why}

The card on the fridge says 212 to 209, and nobody has checked the sums in years. It started with the forks and spread to every rack.

There are points to be had because the makers disagree. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a dish comes clean when its dirty side faces the spray and nothing blocks the way, and everyone agrees on that. Past that, each maker has its own ideas, and each of you has a maker.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away, and check that nothing blocks the dispenser or a spray arm. Run the hot tap first, as Whirlpool says, and lean on the counter while it warms up. That’s where most points are scored.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off food, but don’t rinse. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most detergents use enzymes to break down food. The makers agree, so there’s no point in it, in either sense.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. GE puts platters along the sides and Miele puts very large plates in the middle, so the big plate scores for whoever finds their manual first. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | Points for | The big plate |
> | No points for | Pans face down |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray. The top rack is for small, light and delicate things.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. GE says never put glasses in the bottom rack and Miele says you can. That point went to Miele, and the loser raised both eyebrows, said “oh, that’s good” and asked to see the page. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | Points for | One tall glass |
> | No points for | Plastics on top |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack is a shallow cutlery tray on some machines. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind, which would need a second card.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, and most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, so both sides score every night, which is how the total got so high. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | Points for | Every fork, every night |
> | No points for | Knives point down |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing, under its US name.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays water up through it, so it needs room to turn. |
| So what | A pan handle or ladle hanging through a rack can stop an arm, so turn each arm by hand before you start. The ladle has cost each side a point and now has its own row. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | Points for | Spotting the ladle |
> | No points for | An arm that turns |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves the water. The filter catches food from it. Nobody has ever scored a point on the filter, which may be why it gets cleaned so rarely.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet or powder in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. Use one tablet, in the dispenser, lid closed. |
| So what | Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. Whirlpool prefers tablets and Bosch suggests powder for short cycles, which is good for a point most weeks. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | Points for | Tablet or powder |
> | No points for | Leaving out hand soap |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food. Rinse aid helps water drain off, so dishes dry with fewer spots.

# The card on the fridge {#apart}

> | Year | On the card |
> |---|---|
> | First | Every point initialled and dated, with a manual page next to it. Fourteen to eleven. |
> | Second | A Bosch point crossed out, because Bosch’s other manual says the opposite. Both of you count it as a win. |
> | Third | A column for good reasons, which goes to whoever lost the point and fills up fastest. |
> | Fifth | A week with no points, while one of you was away. The other has written “dull” in the margin. |
> | Eighth | Half under a takeaway menu. Nobody knows the score, both of you are sure you’re ahead, and neither wants to check. |

When advice differs, your dishwasher’s manual wins, and whoever reads it out loud gets the point.
