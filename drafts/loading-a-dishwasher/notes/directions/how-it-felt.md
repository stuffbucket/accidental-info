# What will anyone remember<br/>about the dishwasher? {#why}

Very little about the dishwasher. Nobody can say which way the bowls faced on a Tuesday in March. The kitchen stays with people longer: who was in it, who handed over the plates, and what was said about the forks.

The loading part is short. Water sprays up from an arm under each rack, so a dish comes clean when its dirty side faces the spray and nothing blocks the way. When makers disagree, such as on forks and spoons, your own dishwasher’s manual wins, which leaves the rest of the evening free.

# Where does<br/>everything go? {#fit}

Put each item where water can reach its dirty side and drain away. Check that nothing blocks the detergent dispenser or stops a spray arm turning. That takes a few minutes, and by the weekend nobody will be able to describe the result.

> [!TIP]
> Scrape off large bits of food, but don’t rinse dishes under the tap. Rinsing adds water and energy, and most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down food.

# Bottom rack {#entry num=01 mono=Bo}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The lower of the two main racks. |
| What | The bottom rack holds plates, pots, pans and other large items. Stand plates between the tines facing the middle, and put pans open side down. |
| So what | The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, so the dirtiest things go here. Whoever scrapes the roasting tin without being asked is remembered for longer than the tin. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold big, dirty items |
> | Forgotten by Friday | Which way the plates faced |

## Confused with: Top rack

The bottom rack gets the strongest spray, for heavy and dirty things. The top rack is for small, light and delicate ones.

# Top rack {#entry num=02 mono=Tc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The upper rack, for smaller items. |
| What | The top rack holds cups, glasses, small bowls and plastics. Put them open side down, angled and apart. |
| So what | The top rack usually runs cooler, which keeps plastic from melting. Makers differ on whether glasses can go in the bottom rack, so check your manual before you mention the glass down there, since your manual may be on the glass’s side. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold small, light items |
> | Forgotten by Friday | The order of the mugs |
> | Remembered | Who put the kettle on |

## Confused with: Third rack

A third rack, on machines that have one, is a shallow cutlery tray. Miele says to group the pieces in it by kind.

# Cutlery basket {#entry num=03 mono=Cb}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A basket with sections, in the bottom rack. |
| What | The cutlery basket holds knives, forks and spoons upright. Mix the pieces so they don’t nest, and most makers say knives point down. |
| So what | Makers split on forks and spoons. Miele says handles up is safer and handles down cleans better, and Bosch’s two manuals disagree. A year later, nobody can say which way the spoons went, only how long the talk about them lasted. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Hold cutlery upright |
> | Forgotten by Friday | Handles up or down |
> | Remembered | The fork lecture |

## Confused with: Silverware basket

A silverware basket is the same thing. Many US manuals use that name, so look for either in yours.

# Spray arm {#entry num=04 mono=Ar}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The turning arm under each rack. |
| What | The spray arm turns under a rack and sprays wash water up through it. Turn each arm by hand before you start, to check that it spins. |
| So what | A pan handle hanging through a rack can stop an arm, and that rack comes out dirty. The night it happens is remembered mostly for who laughed first. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Spray water at dishes |
> | Forgotten by Friday | Which cycle ran |
> | Remembered | Who laughed first |

## Confused with: Filter

The spray arm moves water around the dishwasher. The filter catches food from that water and needs cleaning regularly.

# Detergent {#entry num=05 mono=Dg}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A pod, tablet, powder or gel in the door dispenser. |
| What | Most dishwasher detergents use enzymes to break down starch and protein. One tablet or pod is enough, in the dispenser with the lid closed. |
| So what | Never use hand dish soap, which foams too much. Anyone who has tried it once tells the story for years, and the foam gets taller each time. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Job | Break down food |
> | Forgotten by Friday | Pod, tablet or powder |
> | Remembered | The hand soap night |

## Confused with: Rinse aid

Detergent removes food during washing. Rinse aid helps water drain off, which improves drying and reduces spots.

# The usual arguments {#apart}

> | Question | Answer |
> |---|---|
> | Rinse first? | No. Scrape off food and skip the tap. If dishes will sit overnight, run the rinse cycle instead. |
> | Knives, forks and spoons up or down? | Knives point down. Makers split on forks and spoons, so either way is defensible and neither is memorable. |
> | Reload it after them? | Reloading gets noticed, and it’s remembered longer than the plates. A fork facing the other way still gets washed. |
> | What if something bothers me? | Say it once, plainly, with the face you’d use for good news. A sigh at the rack says the same thing and takes longer to forget. |
> | Run the hot tap first? | Whirlpool says yes. The water takes a minute to run hot, which is long enough to lean on the counter and ask how someone’s day went. |

When advice differs, your dishwasher’s manual wins. Nobody will remember which page it was on.
