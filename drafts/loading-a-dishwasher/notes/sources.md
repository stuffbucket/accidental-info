# Sources for An Accidental Guide to… Loading a Dishwasher

All fetched 22 October 2026. Excerpts are verbatim and short. Public, official sources only.

Twelve sources: five dishwasher makers (Bosch ×2 manuals, Miele, Whirlpool, GE Appliances ×2,
Frigidaire), two detergent makers (Cascade, Finish), the US EPA's ENERGY STAR programme (×2) and
one published study. Manual excerpts were read from the PDF text; where the PDF lays text out in
columns, a line break in the PDF is shown here as a space. Pages that would not load and are
NOT used: whirlpool.com blog "proper way to load a dishwasher" and "how to load silverware"
(timed out / access denied), kitchenaid.com "how to load silverware" page and KitchenAid manual
PDF (timed out / blocked), Miele US manual PDFs on us.mieleusa.com (connection refused),
Springer and De Gruyter study pages (login wall / refused).

## 1. Bosch, "Use and Care Manual, Dishwasher SHP78CF5N" (en-us)

<https://media3.bosch-home.com/Documents/9001906312_A.pdf>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none printed on the pages read (PDF file dated 22 October 2024; document 9001906312 A).

- Scrape, don't rinse: "Remove large remnants of food from dishware. To save resources, do not pre-rinse dishware under running water."
- Enzymes: "Modern, powerful detergents mainly use a low-alkaline formulation with enzymes. Enzymes break down starch and remove protein."
- Soil sensors: "The sensors adjust the cycle sequence and strength in the Auto cycles depending on the level and type of soiling."
- Sensor saves water: "If the soiling level is lower, the washing water is used in the next rinse, so reducing water consumption by 3-6 liters."
- Bottom rack: "Arrange pans and plates in the lower rack." "Put heavily soiled dishware in the lower rack, e.g. pans. The more powerful spray jet ensures that the dishwashing result will be better."
- Top rack: "Arrange cups, glasses, and small tableware items in the top rack."
- Face down / angled: "Position containers with the openings facing down so no water can collect in them." "position items with curves or recesses at an angle so water can run off."
- Cutlery: "Arrange the silverware in the silverware basket, ensuring that the items are not nestled together and with the sharp points down."
- Knives (safety): "Load knives and other sharp utensils with their handles up to reduce the risk of cut-type injuries."
- Spray arms: "Do not block the spray arms - make sure that they can turn freely."
- Dispenser lid: "do not block it with dishware so as not to obstruct the lid of the detergent dispenser."
- Detergent amount: "If you are using tablets, one is enough." "1.5 to 1.75 Tbsp (20 ml – 25 ml) detergent is sufficient for normal soiling."
- No hand soap: "Liquid hand soap can cause increased frothing and damage the appliance."
- Rinse aid: "For optimum drying results, use rinse aid."
- Overloading (troubleshooting, "Food remnants on dishware"): "Dishware was placed too close together or rack is overfilled." Fix: "Arrange dishware with sufficient space in between."
- Not dishwasher-safe (table): "Wooden utensils and dishes"; "Plastic parts that are not heat-resistant"; "Dishes made from copper or tin". Also: "Decorated glassware and parts made of aluminum or silver may fade or discolor with dishwashing."
- Filter: "Check the filters for residue after each wash."
- Dishwasher vs hand: "Using the appliance allows you to save energy and water compared to doing dishes by hand."
- Not stated: plates facing the centre; large items at the sides; cast iron; good knives; running the hot tap first.

## 2. Bosch, "Dishwasher Operating Instructions" (en-us, document 9001343203)

<https://media3.bosch-home.com/Documents/9001343203_A.pdf>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none printed (PDF file dated 12 September 2017). Model range not named on the pages read; older Bosch US manual.

- Scrape, don't rinse: "Do not pre-wash items with loosely attached food soil." "Remove all food particles, bones, toothpicks and excessive grease."
- Facing the spray: "Load dishes in the dishwasher racks so the insides of bowls, pots and pans are facing the spray arms."
- No nesting or touching: "Avoid nesting and contact points between dishes."
- Glasses not touching: "Position fragile glassware so that it will not topple over or come in contact with other utensils during the wash cycle."
- Nothing hanging below: "Ensure items do not protrude through the bottom of the racks and block the spray arms."
- Cutlery: "Place knives and sharp utensils with their handles up and forks and spoons with their handles down."
- No hand soap: "Do not use hand dishwashing products in your dishwasher."
- Hot tap first (troubleshooting, long cycle): "Before starting a cycle, run hot water faucet at the sink closest to the dishwasher."
- Iron and wood: "Iron will rust. Hand wash and dry immediately." "Wooden bowls, wooden utensils and utensils with wooden handles can crack, warp and lose their finish."
- Not stated: cast iron by name; good knives.

## 3. Miele, "Operating instructions, Dishwasher" (model G 7106, en-CA)

<https://media.miele.com/downloads/c7/21/00_F61547C6F7151EEDB7BBD8C9070EC721.pdf>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none printed (PDF file dated 5 April 2023; M.-Nr. 11 859 131 / 00).

- Scrape, don't rinse: "Remove coarse food residues from dishware. There is no need to pre-rinse items under running water."
- Why: "There is no need to pre-rinse items under running water. This would unnecessarily increase water and energy consumption."
- Dishwasher vs hand: "Washing in a dishwasher generally uses less water and energy than washing by hand."
- Full but not overloaded: "Make full use of the baskets without overloading the dishwasher."
- No nesting: "Do not place dishware and cutlery inside other pieces where they may be concealed." "Load dishware so that water can access all surfaces."
- Face down, angled: "Hollow items such as cups, glasses, and pots should be placed upside down in the baskets." "Wide based items should be placed at an angle so that water can run off them freely."
- Lower basket: "Place larger and heavier items such as plates, serving platters, saucepans, bowls, etc., in the lower basket." "Place very large plates in the middle of the lower basket."
- Upper basket: "Use the upper basket for small, lightweight, and delicate items such as cups, saucers, glasses, dessert bowls, etc."
- Spray arms: "The spray arms must not be blocked by items which are too tall or hang through the baskets. Test for free movement by manually rotating the spray arms."
- Cutlery basket: "To avoid the risk of injury, knives and forks should be placed with the handles upward in the cutlery basket. However, cutlery placed handle down in the basket will come out cleaner and drier."
- Third-rack cutlery tray: "utensils should be grouped in zones, one for knives, one for forks, one for spoons, etc."
- No hand soap: "Only use detergent and rinse aid formulated for domestic dishwashers. Do not use liquid dish soap."
- Enzymes: "Enzymes to break down starch and loosen protein."
- Amount: "Unless directed otherwise, use one detergent tab or add 20 to 30 ml of detergent to the detergent compartment, depending on the level of soiling of the dishware."
- Dispenser lid: "The detergent must not cause the detergent-compartment lid to get stuck."
- Rinse aid: "Rinse aid is necessary to ensure water does not cling and leave marks on dishware during the drying phase and helps dishware dry faster after it has been washed."
- Not suitable: "Wooden cutlery and dishware or items with wooden parts." "Plastic items that are not heat resistant." "Copper, brass, tin, and aluminum items. These may discolour or become dull."
- Filter: "Clean the filters in the wash cabinet regularly." "The message Check filters will appear in the display after every 50 program sequences."
- Not stated: cast iron; good knives; running the hot tap first.

## 4. Whirlpool, "Getting the Best Results when Using a Dishwasher" (Product Help)

<https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Dishwashers/Product_Info/Dishwasher_Tips_and_Tricks/Getting_the_Best_Results_when_Using_a_Dishwasher>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given ("Last updated" label with no date; footer © 2026).

- Don't rinse: "Do not rinse dishes before putting them into the dishwasher to save water, energy, and time."
- Top rack: "We recommend using the upper rack for bowls and cups. Cups should be placed face down—load items at an angle."
- Bottom rack: "We also recommend using the lower rack for plates, pots/pans, and utensils. Ensure all plates are slotted into the rack on their side."
- Plastics: "Load items at an angle on the top rack." "Plastic items take longer to dry and may not dry completely in a normal dry cycle, so let them air dry."
- Cutlery: "Mix different types of silverware to keep them separate. Load knives down, forks up, and alternate spoons for best cleaning results."
- Detergent: "High-quality pre-measured tablets and packs are recommended for improved performance." "Always place pre-measured detergents in the main compartment and close the lid." "Use automatic dishwasher detergent only."
- Hot tap first: "run the hot water faucet at the sink closest to the dishwasher until the water is hot" and "If the water temperature is too low, loads may not wash well."
- Rinse aid: "Rinse aid can improve drying and reduce water spots and filming."
- Not stated: scraping; spray arms; overloading; filter; soil sensors; what not to wash (only "Ensure all dishes are labeled as "Dishwasher Safe."").

## 5. GE Appliances, "Dishwasher - Loading Tips"

<https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=19573>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given.

- Scrape, don't rinse: "There is no need to pre-rinse dishes before loading. Just scrape off large pieces of food."
- Angle and don't stack: "It is important to angle all items for proper drainage" and "Ensure dishes are not stacked up on top of each other."
- Spray arm: "Make sure the spray arm can rotate freely."
- Top rack: "We recommend loading glasses, cups, saucers, dishwasher-safe plastic and wood items in the upper rack of your Dishwasher."
- Facing the centre (said under the upper-rack heading): "When loading any item, it should face toward the water source in the center."
- Plates: "Place plates and saucers in between the tines."
- Large items: "Platters should be loaded along the sides, in the corners or in the back."
- Glasses: "Never place glasses in the lower rack."
- Pots face down: "pots, pans and casserole dishes should be loaded in the lower rack with the open end down, facing directly toward the wash arm below the lower rack."
- Cutlery: "Load silverware in the basket with handles facing down."
- Not stated: detergent; what not to wash; hot tap; filter.

## 6. GE Appliances, "Dishwasher - Proper Silverware Loading"

<https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=18915>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given.

- Forks and knives: "Place forks and knives with the handles up to protect hands."
- Spoons: "Place spoon handles down if there is an unusually large load of silverware or utensils in the basket. Otherwise, place spoon handles up also."
- Mix, don't nest: "Best washing results are obtained when the silverware is mixed and evenly distributed, not nested together."
- Spray arm and dispenser: "Slender items must not extend through the bottom of the basket as this may prevent the wash arm from rotating. Tall cutlery must not be placed in front of the basket where they may prevent the detergent cup from opening."
- Silver: "Do not mix sterling silver with stainless in your silverware basket."
- Third rack: "Do not allow cutlery of different materials to touch each other in the third rack."

## 7. Frigidaire (Electrolux), "Dishwasher - Loading Guide"

<https://owner.frigidaire.com/support-articles/article/1834625-dishwasher-loading-guide?locale=en-US>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given.

- Bottom rack: "The lower rack is best for plates, saucers, bowls, and cookware."
- Large items at the edge: "Large items should be placed along the edge so they do not block the spray arm rotation."
- Bowls and pans: "place bowls, casseroles, and sauce pans with soiled surface facing down or toward the center"
- Top rack: "Load glasses and plastic items in upper rack only." "Load items with open end facing down for better cleaning and draining."
- Not touching: "Damage may occur if delicate items touch each other during dishwasher operation."
- Cutlery: "mix items in each section of the basket with some pointing up and some down to avoid nesting." "Water spray cannot reach nested items."
- Silver: "Do not mix silver and stainless to avoid damaging the silver finish."
- Spray arm check: "Make sure the loaded items do not interfere with the rotation of the spray arm. Check this by rotating the spray arm by hand." "Be sure nothing protrudes through bottom of basket or rack to block the spray arm."
- Dispenser: "Load racks so that large items do not prevent the detergent dispenser from opening."
- A pre-rinse exception (for staining foods like mustard, vinegar, tomato, if the load will wait): "it is best to pre-rinse or use the RINSE cycle to rinse off these food soils"
- Not stated: scraping; knife direction; overloading; what not to wash.

<https://cascadeclean.com/en-us/how-to/lessons-on-loading/how-to-load-a-dishwasher/>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given (footer © 2026 Procter & Gamble).

- Scrape: "scrape whatever stuck-on food you can into the trash"
- Skip the pre-rinse (with its own product): "If you're using a premium ActionPacs™ , there's no need to pre-wash dishes before you place them in the dishwasher."
- Enzymes: "Special enzymes in each pac latch on, break down, and wash away food"
- Bottom rack, facing the centre: "Put plates and messy, burnt-on pots and pans on the bottom rack facing the water source." "Typically, the water source is in the center of the machine"
- Top rack: "Place cups, mugs, and bowls facedown on the top rack." "Plasticware also belongs on the top rack." "Temperatures typically remain cooler on the top rack, which will prevent plastic from melting."
- Cutlery: "You don't want your spoons to spoon, so carefully load utensils to prevent nesting" and "Load knives and other cutlery with the sharp end down."
- Detergent: "drop one ActionPac into the dry detergent compartment and fill up the rinse aid dispenser"
- Not stated: overloading; hot tap; filter; spray arms by name.

## 10. ENERGY STAR (US EPA), "Dishwashers"

<https://www.energystar.gov/products/dishwashers>
Checked: 22 October 2026. Page date: none given (current specification effective July 19, 2023).

- Don't rinse, scrape: "Rinsing dishes before loading them in the dishwasher increases the total water and energy used." "Save yourself the rinsing - just scrape food off dishes."
- If dishes wait: "If your dirty dishes sit overnight, use your dishwasher's rinse feature. It uses a fraction of the water needed to hand rinse."
- Vs hand washing: "A new ENERGY STAR certified dishwasher uses less than half as much energy as washing dishes by hand and saves 8,400 gallons of water each year!"
- Full loads: "Dishwashers use about the same amount of energy and water regardless of the number of dishes inside" so "run full loads whenever possible."
- Lifetime figure: "can save you about 5,800 gallons of water over its lifetime" (the page does not say compared with what).
- Not stated: a gallons figure for pre-rinsing; gallons per hand-washed load.

## Where sources disagree

- Cutlery, handles up or down. Everyone agrees sharp knives go point down and pieces shouldn't nest. Beyond that:
  - Miele: knives and forks handles up "To avoid the risk of injury", while admitting "cutlery placed handle down in the basket will come out cleaner and drier."
  - Bosch (SHP78CF5N, current): all silverware "with the sharp points down".
  - Bosch (older 9001343203 manual): knives handles up, "forks and spoons with their handles down."
  - Whirlpool: "Load knives down, forks up, and alternate spoons". ("Forks up" most likely means tines up, i.e. handles down; the page doesn't say.)
  - GE contradicts itself: the Loading Tips page says "Load silverware in the basket with handles facing down"; the Silverware page says forks and knives handles up, and spoons handles up too unless the basket is very full.
  - Frigidaire: "some pointing up and some down to avoid nesting."
  - Cascade: "knives and other cutlery with the sharp end down."
  - So: handles down cleans better (Miele says so outright); handles up is safer (Miele, GE, Bosch). Bosch's own two manuals differ. Tie-breaker: your own manual.
- Large items: GE puts platters "along the sides, in the corners or in the back" and Frigidaire puts large items "along the edge"; Miele says "Place very large plates in the middle of the lower basket."
- Glasses in the bottom rack: GE "Never place glasses in the lower rack"; Frigidaire "upper rack only"; Miele says glasses "can also be placed in the lower basket."
- Wood: GE recommends "dishwasher-safe plastic and wood items" for the upper rack; Bosch lists "Wooden utensils and dishes" as not dishwasher-safe and Miele lists wooden items as not suitable.
- Pre-rinsing: all makers and ENERGY STAR say don't. Exceptions: Frigidaire says pre-rinse or use the RINSE cycle for staining foods if the load will wait; ENERGY STAR says use the rinse feature if dishes sit overnight. Cascade ties its no-rinse advice to "premium ActionPacs".
- How much water a pre-rinse wastes: only the detergent makers give a number. Finish says "up to 20 gallons" and also says "per load"; it cites EPA data in general but names no page. ENERGY STAR's dishwasher pages give no gallons figure for pre-rinsing.
- Filter: Bosch says check it "after each wash"; Miele's machine prompts a check "after every 50 program sequences".
- Detergent type: Whirlpool recommends tablets and packs over powder, liquid or gel; Bosch says tabs "sometimes" don't dissolve fully on shorter cycles and recommends powder for shorter cycles.
