# Sources for An Accidental Guide to… The Last Move in Chess

All fetched 22 October 2026 (raw files saved outside the repo while writing). Excerpts are
verbatim and short. Public, official material from FIDE (the international chess federation)
and US Chess only.

## 1. FIDE Laws of Chess (taking effect from 1 January 2023)

FIDE Handbook, E.01 "FIDE Laws of Chess taking effect from 1 January 2023".
<https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023>
Page date: "Applied from 01/01/2023". The handbook's list shows no later edition as of
22 October 2026.

The goal, check, checkmate:

- 1.4 "The objective of each player is to place the opponent's king ‘under attack’ in such a
  way that the opponent has no legal move."
- 1.4.1 "The player who achieves this goal is said to have ‘checkmated’ the opponent's king
  and to have won the game. Leaving one's own king under attack, exposing one's own king to
  attack and also ’capturing’ the opponent's king is not allowed."
- 3.9.1 "The king is said to be 'in check' if it is attacked by one or more of the
  opponent's pieces"
- 3.9.2 "No piece can be moved that will either expose the king of the same colour to check
  or leave that king in check."
- 5.1.1 "The game is won by the player who has checkmated his/her opponent's king. This
  immediately ends the game"

Resigning:

- 5.1.2 "The game is lost by the player who declares he/she resigns (this immediately ends
  the game), unless the position is such that the opponent cannot checkmate the player's king
  by any possible series of legal moves. In this case the result of the game is a draw."

Losing on time (Competitive Rules, Article 6):

- 6.9 "if a player does not complete the prescribed number of moves in the allotted time, the
  game is lost by that player. However, the game is drawn if the position is such that the
  opponent cannot checkmate the player's king by any possible series of legal moves."

Draws:

- 5.2.1 "The game is drawn when the player to move has no legal move and his/her king is not
  in check. The game is said to end in ‘stalemate’. This immediately ends the game"
- 5.2.2 "The game is drawn when a position has arisen in which neither player can checkmate
  the opponent's king with any series of legal moves. The game is said to end in a ‘dead
  position’."
- 5.2.3 "The game is drawn upon agreement between the two players during the game, provided
  both players have made at least one move."
- 9.1.2.1 "A player wishing to offer a draw shall do so after having made a move on the
  chessboard and before pressing his/her clock." ... "the offer cannot be withdrawn and
  remains valid until the opponent accepts it, rejects it orally, rejects it by touching a
  piece with the intention of moving or capturing it, or the game is concluded in some other
  way."
- 9.2 Repetition: "The game is drawn, upon a correct claim by a player having the move, when
  the same position for at least the third time (not necessarily by a repetition of moves)"
- 9.3.2 Fifty moves: "the last 50 moves by each player have been completed without the
  movement of any pawn and without any capture."
- 9.6.1 and 9.6.2: drawn without a claim when "the same position has appeared ... at least
  five times" or "any series of at least 75 moves have been made by each player without the
  movement of any pawn and without any capture."
- 10.1 Points: "a player who wins his/her game, or wins by forfeit, scores one point (1), a
  player who loses his/her game, or forfeits, scores no points (0), and a player who draws
  his/her game scores a half point (½)."

Touch-move and adjusting:

- 4.1 "Each move must be played with one hand only."
- 4.2.1 "Only the player having the move may adjust one or more pieces on their squares,
  provided that he/she first expresses his/her intention (for example by saying “j'adoube” or
  “I adjust”)."
- 4.3.1 "one or more of his/her own pieces, he/she must move the first piece touched that can
  be moved."
- 4.3.2 "one or more of his/her opponent's pieces, he/she must capture the first piece
  touched that can be captured."
- 4.7 "When, as a legal move or part of a legal move, a piece has been released on a square,
  it cannot be moved to another square on this move."
- Articles 1 to 5 sit under the heading "BASIC RULES OF PLAY"; clocks, recording and
  arbiters come under "COMPETITIVE RULES OF PLAY" from Article 6.

Conduct:

- 11.5 "It is forbidden to distract or annoy the opponent in any manner whatsoever. This
  includes unreasonable claims, unreasonable offers of a draw or the introduction of a
  source of noise into the playing area."

Notation (Appendix C): "+ = check", "++ or # = checkmate", "(=)" marks a draw offer.

## 2. US Chess, Official Rules of Chess, 7th edition (free chapters, revised 1 January 2026)

US Chess Federation, "Official Rules of Chess, 7th Edition, Free download version: chapters
1, 2, 9, 10, 11 only", revised January 1, 2026.
<https://new.uschess.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/us-chess-rule-book-online-2026.pdf>

- 13B Resignation: "The player whose opponent resigns wins the game. This immediately ends
  the game. Saying I resign or tipping over the king are relatively clear ways to resign."
- "Stopping both clocks does not necessarily indicate a resignation."
- "Likewise, the offer of a handshake is not necessarily a resignation. On occasion, one
  player believes the handshake agrees to a draw while the other interprets it as a
  resignation."
- 14A Stalemate: "The game is drawn when the king of the player to move is not in check and
  that player has no legal move. This type of draw is called stalemate." "Note that it is
  incorrect to refer to all drawn games as stalemate."
- 13A: "The player who checkmates the opponent's king, providing the mating move is legal,
  wins the game. This immediately ends the game."

## 3. FIDE, Curriculum: Chess for Refugees

FIDE (World Chess Federation), "Curriculum Chess for Refugees" (PDF created 9 October 2023).
<https://www.fide.com/docs/presentations/Curriculum%20Chess%20for%20Refugees.pdf>

- Getting out of check: "The ways to get out of check are to move the king, protect the
  check with another piece or to capture the checking piece."
- Blocking: "PROTECT the check only works if the checking piece is a queen, rook, or bishop
  and there is at least one empty square in the line between the checking piece and the
  checked king."
- Checkmate: "When you cannot get out of the check, it is CHECKMATE and this ends the game."
- Stalemate: "If the king is not in check, but no piece can be moved without putting the king
  in check, then the game will end with a stalemate draw."
- Ways to draw, for beginners: "three times repetition of moves or position", "perpetual
  check", "applying 50-moves rule", "insufficient material for checkmate (example: bishop or
  knight and king against naked king)", "agreement", "stalemate".
- Handshake: "chess players shake hands before and after a game of chess." and "No matter
  what the outcome of the game was, don't forget to respect your opponent. Shake hands, share
  your thoughts about the game. Don't forget to put pieces back to their starting position."
- Draw offer in class: "The player whose turn it is makes his/her move and says: "I offer a
  draw" and hit the clock."

## 4. US Chess, Guide to Scholastic Chess (11th edition)

US Chess Federation, "Guide to Scholastic Chess", 11th edition (file dated 29 June 2021).
<https://new.uschess.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/11th-edition-guide-to-scholastic-chess-6-29-21.pdf>

- "Move the piece you touch; touch only the piece you are sure you wish to move."
- "Emphasize that your club plays “touch move.” It is an important rule to follow from the
  beginning."
- "Make your move before offering a draw."
- "remember, stalemate or checkmate immediately stops the clock and ends the game."
- "Touch your king first when castling."

## The two board positions in the overview

Built from the rules above and checked square by square (no source states these exact
positions; they are worked examples of FIDE 3.9, 5.1.1 and 5.2.1):

- Checkmate: White king g6, White queen g7, Black king h8, Black to move. The queen on g7
  attacks h8 (diagonal), so Black is in check. The king can't take the queen, because the
  White king on g6 guards g7. g8 and h7 are attacked by the queen. No Black piece can capture
  or block. So it's checkmate.
- Stalemate: White king g6, White queen f7, Black king h8, Black to move. h8 isn't attacked
  (the queen on f7 reaches g8, g7 and h7, not h8), so Black isn't in check. g8 is attacked by
  the queen, g7 and h7 by the queen and king. Black has no other piece. So Black has no legal
  move and isn't in check: stalemate.
