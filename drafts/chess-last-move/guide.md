# How does a<br/>chess game end? {#why}

A chess game ends in a win or a draw. You win by checkmate, or when the other player
resigns. With a clock, a player who runs out of time usually loses. A draw comes from
agreement, stalemate or one of a few other rules.

A win scores one point, a draw half a point each and a loss nothing. The game ends as soon
as checkmate, stalemate or a resignation happens, so nobody ever captures a king.

Check is the only one of these five that doesn’t end the game.

> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | Check | A king is under attack, so its player must respond. |
> | Checkmate | A king is in check with no legal way out. |
> | Stalemate | The player to move has no legal move, but that player’s king isn’t in check. |
> | Draw | The game ends without a winner. |
> | Resigning | A player concedes the game. |

# Checkmate or<br/>stalemate? {#fit}

```diagram
board Checkmate | 7k/6Q1/6K1/8/8/8/8/8 | Black to move. The queen gives check, and the white king guards the queen.
mark Mt h8
board Stalemate | 7k/5Q2/6K1/8/8/8/8/8 | Black to move. The king isn’t in check, but every square next to it is attacked.
mark Sm h8
```

Black has no legal move on either board. On the left, that’s checkmate and White wins. On
the right, it’s stalemate and the game is a draw. When you’re winning, leave your opponent a
legal move unless your move is checkmate.

# Check {#entry num=01 mono=Kc}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A piece attacking a king, or a + written after the move. |
| What | Check means the king is attacked by an enemy piece. |
| So what | You must get out of check at once: move the king, block the attack or capture the attacker. No move may leave your own king in check. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Result | The game goes on |
> | Decided by | The position after the move |

## Confused with: Checkmate

Check means the king is attacked. Checkmate means there is no legal way to stop the
attack.

# Checkmate {#entry num=02 mono=Mt}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | The king in check, with no legal move that gets out. |
| What | Checkmate is check with no legal way out. None of the three ways out of check works. |
| So what | Checkmate ends the game at once. The player who gave checkmate wins, and # after the move records it. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Result | A win |
> | Decided by | The checkmating move |

## Confused with: Stalemate

Checkmate requires the king to be in check. In stalemate, the king isn’t in check, but its
player has no legal move.

# Stalemate {#entry num=03 mono=Sm}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | Your turn, your king not in check and no legal move. |
| What | Stalemate is a position where the player to move has no legal move and isn’t in check. |
| So what | Stalemate makes the game a draw, however many pieces the other side has. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Result | A draw |
> | Decided by | The position |

## Confused with: Any draw

Stalemate is one kind of draw. Not every draw is a stalemate.

# Draw {#entry num=04 mono=Dw}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | A draw offer after a move, or the same position for the third time. |
| What | A draw is a game that ends without a winner. A draw comes from agreement, stalemate, a position where nobody can checkmate, or a claim after repetition or fifty moves each without a pawn move or capture. |
| So what | The same position three times lets the player to move claim a draw. Under FIDE rules, five times ends the game in a draw. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Result | Half a point each |
> | Decided by | The rules or both players |

## Confused with: A handshake

A handshake alone doesn’t state the result. One player may take it as a draw and the other
as a resignation, so say the result in words.

# Resigning {#entry num=05 mono=Rs}

| Field | Text |
|---|---|
| Identify by | “I resign” said aloud, or a king tipped over. |
| What | Resigning means giving up the game, which ends at once. |
| So what | The other player wins when you resign. Under FIDE rules, if the other player couldn’t possibly checkmate, it’s a draw instead. |

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer |
> |---|---|
> | Result | A loss, usually |
> | Decided by | The player resigning |

## Confused with: Stopping the clock

Stopping both clocks doesn’t necessarily mean a player has resigned. Ask before you treat
the game as over.

# How do you<br/>finish politely? {#habits}

A few habits prevent confusion at the end.

> | Habit | Why |
> |---|---|
> | Touch only the intended piece | Touch your own piece and you must move it. Touch an opponent’s piece and you must take it, if you can. |
> | Say “I adjust” first | On your turn, say it before you straighten a piece, so your opponent knows you aren’t moving it. |
> | Offer a draw after moving | Make your move, offer the draw, then press your clock if you have one. The offer stands until your opponent accepts, says no or touches a piece. |
> | Resign clearly | Say “I resign” or tip over your king, so the result is clear. |
> | Shake hands before and after | A handshake shows respect. Words make the result clear. |
> | Put the pieces back | Return each piece to its starting square. |

After the handshake, share your thoughts about the game, whatever the result.
