Checked and clean: the light claims. The guide never says which window is brightest or how
many feet light reaches, so the sources' disagreement (Missouri: south brightest; Iowa State:
south or east; RHS: south or west in winter; Iowa State "a few feet" against Missouri "10

light the top limit on growth) follows the "Where sources disagree" note exactly. The pot,
drainage, gravel, saucer, outer-pot and root-rot rescue claims (lines 85-86, 98, 105-106, 130)

## Wrong

### W1. Soil "pulled from the pot" (line 30, Thirsty or drowning table)

**Guide:** "| Soil | Dry, sometimes pulled from the pot | Wet or soggy |"

**Source** (University of Maryland Extension, Overwatered Indoor Plants,
https://extension.umd.edu/resource/overwatered-indoor-plants): "the media has pulled away from
the sides of the pot"; (Clemson HGIC, Indoor Plants – Watering,
https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/): "it shrinks away from the sides of
the pot."

**Verdict:** wrong as written. The sources describe dry soil shrinking away from the pot's
sides, not soil pulled out of the pot. Minor, but the cell reads as a different sign.

**Corrected:** "| Soil | Dry, may pull away from the sides | Wet or soggy |"

## Overreach

### O1. Which leaves go first (line 32, Thirsty or drowning table)

**Guide:** "| First leaves to go | Older, lower leaves | Old and new leaves together |"

**Source** (Royal Horticultural Society, How to help a poorly houseplant,
https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/how-to-help-a-poorly-houseplant): "older leaves
at the base of a dry plant will often yellow and fall first." (University of Maryland
Extension, Overwatered Indoor Plants, https://extension.umd.edu/resource/overwatered-indoor-plants):
"The primary symptom of excess moisture is wilting or yellowing of lower and inner leaves."

**Verdict:** overreach. The RHS hedges with "often", and UMD says overwatering also hits lower
leaves first, so this row is not a clean dividing line. (The RHS also lists lower leaves
yellowing and falling as a low-light sign.) The cells state it as a rule.

**Corrected:** "| First leaves to go | Often lowest, oldest | Often old and new at once |"

### O2. Alive or dead stated as certain, from an outdoor-shrub source (lines 124 and 126, Is it dead yet?)

**Guide:** "| Check | Still alive | Dead |" and "| Scratch a woody stem | Green or cream and moist | Brown and brittle |"

**Source** (N.C. Cooperative Extension, Should I Replace Plants That Died in the 2026 Drought?,
https://beaufort.ces.ncsu.edu/news/should-i-replace-plants-that-died-in-the-2026-drought/):
"Brown, tan, brittle tissue indicates that portion of the stem is dead." / "If the outer twigs
are dead but green tissue appears farther down, the plant may recover" / "A firm crown with
white, cream, or pale green tissue may still be alive. A crown that is soft, hollow, blackened,
or foul-smelling is unlikely to recover."

**Verdict:** overreach. The only source for these checks is a county article about outdoor
shrubs and perennials, and it hedges every result ("may still be alive", "unlikely to
recover"). A brown scratch shows that spot is dead, not the plant. The column headers turn
signs into verdicts. Limiting the scratch test to woody stems is right and should stay.

**Corrected:** line 124 "| Check | Good sign | Bad sign |"; line 126 "| Scratch a woody stem low | Green or cream, moist | Brown, brittle |"

### O3. Winter always means less water (line 66, Watering)

**Guide:** "Cool rooms and winter mean less often."

**Source** (Royal Horticultural Society, How to grow houseplants,
https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/growing-guide): "Plants in a cool room will
need watering less often than plants in a warm room" / "Pots dry out faster in winter when the
atmospheric humidity is lowered by central heating". (Clemson HGIC,
https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/): "while it is dormant, a plant will
need less water."

**Verdict:** overreach. Cool rooms and slow or dormant growth mean less water, but the same RHS
page says heated rooms dry pots faster in winter. "Winter" on its own is not reliable.

**Corrected:** "Cool rooms and slow growth need less."

### O4. Rotten roots "sour-smelling" (lines 34 and 114)

**Guide:** line 34 "| Roots | Usually firm and light-coloured | Brown or black, soft, sour-smelling |"; line 114 "> | Trouble | Dark, soft or sour-smelling |"

**Source** (University of Florida, Diagnosing houseplants 101,
https://epi.ufl.edu/2024/07/03/diagnosing-houseplants-101-is-your-plant-diseased-or-just-overwatered/):
"The soil may also develop a sour, stinky smell"; (University of Maryland Extension, Diagnose
Indoor Plant Problems, https://extension.umd.edu/resource/diagnose-indoor-plant-problems):
"Diseased roots will appear dark and soft."

**Verdict:** overreach. UF says the soil may smell sour, not the roots, and sources.md records
that UMD does not mention smell. Dark and soft is the supported sign.

**Corrected:** line 34 Drowning cell "Brown or black, and soft"; line 114 "> | Trouble | Dark and soft |"

### O5. Roots of a thirsty plant (line 34, Thirsty or drowning table)

**Guide:** "| Roots | Usually firm and light-coloured | Brown or black, soft, sour-smelling |"

**Source** (University of Maryland Extension, Diagnose Indoor Plant Problems,
https://extension.umd.edu/resource/diagnose-indoor-plant-problems): "Healthy roots should be
light-colored and firm."

**Verdict:** overreach. The sources describe healthy roots, not the roots of a dry plant. A
badly dried plant may not have healthy roots.

**Corrected:** Thirsty cell "Light and firm if healthy"

### O6. Wet soil plus water "makes things worse" (line 15, Is my houseplant dying?)

**Guide:** "If the soil is already wet, more water makes things worse."

**Source** (Clemson HGIC, https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/indoor-plants-watering/): "under
these conditions, watering may make the problem worse."

**Verdict:** overreach, minor. The source says "may", and about roots that already can't take
up water.

**Corrected:** "If the soil is already wet, more water can make it worse."

### O7. "Probably isn't dying" (line 13, Is my houseplant dying?)

**Guide:** "Your houseplant probably isn’t dying yet."

**Source** (Royal Horticultural Society, How to help a poorly houseplant,
https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/types/houseplants/how-to-help-a-poorly-houseplant): "Many
houseplants will recover quickly once their growing conditions are improved"

**Verdict:** overreach, minor. The source says many recover, not that a given struggling plant
is probably not dying. The rest of the paragraph already carries the reassurance.

**Corrected:** "Your houseplant may not be dying."
