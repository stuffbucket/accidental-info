## Is my houseplant dying? (lines 13 to 19)

2. Lines 13 to 15, one paragraph: Your houseplant probably isn’t dying yet. Wilting, yellow leaves and leaf drop are common signs of trouble, and many houseplants recover once their conditions improve. Watering is the most common problem, and light is what most often holds a plant back. Both can be fixed.
3. Lines 17 to 19, one paragraph: A drooping plant can be thirsty, with too little water, or drowning, with too much. The signs overlap, so check the soil before you reach for the watering can. If the soil is already wet, more water makes things worse.

## Light (lines 49 to 63)

9. Line 49: | What | Light is what a plant uses to grow. Light drops quickly as you move away from a window, and winter light is weaker. |
10. Line 50: | So what | Too little light gives pale, weak, leaning growth, and too much leaves scorched patches. In winter, move plants closer to a bright window. |
11. Lines 62 to 63, one paragraph: Light changes how a plant grows. Warmth changes how fast the soil dries, so a plant in a warm, sunny spot needs water more often.

## Watering (lines 70 to 84)

12. Line 70: | What | Watering is when and how much you water. The right timing depends on the plant, the light, the room and the season. |
13. Line 71: | So what | When the top inch or so of soil is dry, water until it runs out of the bottom, then empty the saucer. Cool rooms and winter mean less often. |
14. Line 79: > | Trouble | Always dry or always wet |
15. Lines 83 to 84, one paragraph: Watering wets the soil, where the roots are. Misting wets only the leaves.

## Pot (lines 91 to 105)

16. Line 91: | What | A pot holds the soil and roots. A drainage hole lets extra water out, so air stays around the roots. |
17. Line 92: | So what | Every pot needs a drainage hole, and gravel in the bottom doesn’t replace one. A pot left standing in water can drown its roots. |
18. Lines 104 to 105, one paragraph: A plant pot has a drainage hole. A decorative outer pot can hide standing water, so tip it out after watering.

## Roots (lines 111 to 126)

19. Line 111: | Identify by | What you see when you slide the plant out of its pot. |
20. Line 112: | What | Roots take up water, and they need air too. In soil that stays wet, roots run short of air, rot and stop taking up water. |
21. Line 113: | So what | Rotting roots make a plant wilt even in wet soil. Cut out the rotten parts and repot the healthy sections. |
22. Line 120: > | Healthy | White or light, and firm |
23. Line 121: > | Trouble | Dark, soft or sour-smelling |
24. Lines 125 to 126, one paragraph: Soil holds the water. Roots take it up, so wet soil can still surround roots that can’t drink.
