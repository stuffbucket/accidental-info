# Sources for An Accidental Guide to… Grilled Cheese

All read on 22 October 2026. Excerpts are verbatim and short. Public culinary references
only: Food Network Kitchen's test-kitchen guides and recipes, Epicurious and King Arthur
Baking. No food-safety facts are needed.

## 1. The method, heat, timing, cheese, bread, mayonnaise, lid

Food Network Kitchen (Layla Khoury-Hanold), "A Precise Guide to Making Perfect Grilled
Cheese Sandwiches". Food Network, updated 8 April 2026.
<https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/how-to-make-grilled-cheese>

- Heat and time: "Preheat a skillet over medium-low heat, then cook the assembled sandwich uncovered…" "…until both sides are golden, about 3 to 4 minutes per side."
- Where the fat goes in this recipe: "Our perfect ratio: 1 tablespoon fat total for each sandwich, spread or brushed on the outside of each slice ."
- Lid: "(Don’t cover the pan with a lid, as this will produce a soggy, slightly steamed sandwich.)"
- Cheese: "So it makes sense that the best cheese for grilled cheese is a melting cheese like American, sharp cheddar and Swiss."
- Cheese: "…such as spicy Pepper Jack, creamy Provolone or nutty Gruyere."
- Cheese to skip: "Save hard or aged cheeses such as Parmesan and Manchego for cheese boards…" "…reserve feta and goat cheese for finishing salads or pasta."
- Shredded cheese: "It tends to melt faster than sliced cheese, which ensures that all the cheese melts before the bread finishes toasting."
- Mayonnaise: "Mayo has a higher smoke point than butter, which guarantees that the bread gets golden-brown and crisp without burning."
- Bread (summary from the fetch, not verbatim): sliced white bread is the usual choice; sourdough, multigrain, potato bread, brioche and challah also work; airy breads such as focaccia let cheese seep through and burn. Verbatim: "Sliced white bread is a go-to for grilled cheese sandwiches", and of airy bread, "your cheese might seep through the bread and possibly burn in the pan."
- Panini press method: "place sandwich in press, close the top" (medium heat, about 5 to 6 minutes; summary from the fetch).
- Not stated: pressing the sandwich in a pan, resting before cutting.
- The guide's "about 1 tablespoon" of butter per sandwich is this source's ratio for fat on the bread, applied to the pan. The guide says so on the arguments page ("the same amount").

## 2. Butter in the pan, medium-low heat, cheese melted before the bread browns

Food Network Kitchen, "Grilled Cheese Sandwiches" (from How to Boil Water). Food Network,
no page date.
<https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/grilled-cheese-sandwiches-recipe2-1944002>

- "Heat a large skillet over medium-low heat."
- Butter in the pan: "Melt another tablespoon of the butter in the skillet; swirl around to coat the pan."
- After the flip: "add a small hazelnut-size piece of butter to the pan"
- This recipe also butters the bread: "generously butter the top sides of each slice", placed "buttered side down, in the skillet".
- First side: "Cook, without pressing, until the bread browns and the cheese is slightly melted, about 5 minutes."
- Second side: "about 3 to 4 minutes more"
- The aim: "melt the cheese completely before the sandwiches are brown"
- Heat: "adjusting the heat as needed"

## 3. Butter in the pan instead of on the bread

Tommy Werner, "How to Make Crispy, Butter-Basted Grilled Cheese". Epicurious, 19 January
2016.
<https://www.epicurious.com/expert-advice/extra-crispy-grilled-cheese-tip-article>

- "Instead of buttering the outside of the bread (which is how I’ve always done it), Keller’s recipe calls for browning butter , in a pan and sliding the sandwich in."
- Basting: "I added a few tablespoons of butter (enough to scoop and spoon over the sandwich) into the pan, tilted the pan towards me, and basted the sandwich with the now-melted butter for 30 seconds. The bread soaked up the butter like a champ, getting extra crisp and golden and, well, buttery."
- Used only for the two facts: butter can go in the pan instead of on the bread, and bread
  in a buttered pan soaks up the butter. The chef's name stays out of the guide.

## 4. Mayonnaise, heat by cheese, a lid

PJ Hamel, "5 tips for the best grilled cheese ever". King Arthur Baking, 19 March 2015.
<https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2015/03/19/grilled-cheese>

- "Spread the bread with mayonnaise, rather than butter."
- "Keep the butter/mayo; even the thinnest application will work magic."
- "The rate at which your cheese melts will determine the temperature of your pan." "For cheeses that are slower to melt, use lower heat."
- Lid: "And cover the pan, to create a small oven" … "the trapped heat will help melt the cheese more quickly than simple surface heat."
- "full-fat cheese will melt more quickly than lower-fat cheese."

## Where sources disagree (the arguments)

- Butter on the bread or in the pan: Food Network's guide puts the fat on the outside of
  each slice. Food Network's How to Boil Water recipe does both. The Epicurious method
  browns butter in the pan and slides the sandwich in. The guide states the pan method and
  says buttering the bread works too.
- Butter or mayonnaise: King Arthur and Food Network both offer mayonnaise; Food Network
  says it has a higher smoke point than butter, so it browns without burning.
- Lid: King Arthur covers the pan to melt the cheese. Food Network says a lid makes a
  soggy, steamed sandwich. The guide gives both.
- Time per side: 3 to 4 minutes (Food Network guide) or about 5 minutes then 3 to 4
  (How to Boil Water). The guide says 3 to 5 minutes, until golden.

## Worked numbers

- 1 tablespoon of fat per sandwich (Food Network) split between the two sides is about half
  a tablespoon per side.
