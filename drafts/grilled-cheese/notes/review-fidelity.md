## The butter question

**Is the butter-in-the-pan method fairly supported?** In principle, yes. In its numbers,
only partly.

- Pan instead of bread: Epicurious supports it directly. "Instead of buttering the outside of
  the bread (which is how I’ve always done it), Keller’s recipe calls for browning butter, in
  a pan and sliding the sandwich in." The soak claim at lines 90 and 130 also comes from
  here. "The bread soaked up the butter like a champ."
- Butter in the pan plus medium-low heat: the How to Boil Water recipe melts "another
  tablespoon of the butter in the skillet" and swirls it. That recipe also butters the bread
  ("buttered side down"), so it supports pan butter, not pan butter alone.
- Medium-low heat, uncovered, 3 to 5 minutes a side, cheese melted before the bread gets too
  dark: well supported by both Food Network pages. None of this depends on where the butter
  goes.
- The amount is the weak link. "1 tablespoon fat total for each sandwich" is Food Network’s
  ratio for fat "spread or brushed on the outside of each slice". The only pan-only test
  (Epicurious) used "a few tablespoons of butter" and basted for 30 seconds. No source
  melts half a tablespoon per side in the pan with the bread unbuttered. See O1.

**Is the butter-the-bread camp fairly represented?** Fairly, but thinly. It gets one
sentence at line 130, "Buttering the outside of the bread works." It has the most support
of the three butter methods: it is the main method of the Food Network test-kitchen guide,
the How to Boil Water recipe uses it alongside pan butter, and King Arthur spreads its fat
(mayonnaise) on the bread too. The guide’s own 1 tablespoon figure is borrowed from that
camp. Nothing in the guide is unfair to it, but the line at 14 to 15 reads as the only way
browning happens. See F1 and F2.

## Overreach

### O1. Half a tablespoon per side in the pan (lines 29, 35, 38, 91 and 97, method and Butter)

**Guide:** "Use about 1 tablespoon of butter for each sandwich." (line 29) and "One tablespoon
per sandwich gives each side half a tablespoon." (line 91)

**Source** (Food Network Kitchen, A Precise Guide to Making Perfect Grilled Cheese
Sandwiches, https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/food-network-essentials/how-to-make-grilled-cheese):
"Our perfect ratio: 1 tablespoon fat total for each sandwich, spread or brushed on the
outside of each slice." And (Epicurious, https://www.epicurious.com/expert-advice/extra-crispy-grilled-cheese-tip-article):
"I added a few tablespoons of butter (enough to scoop and spoon over the sandwich) into the
pan".

**Verdict:** overreach, moderate. The ratio is for fat spread on the bread. The guide moves
it into the pan without saying so. The one pan-only source used several tablespoons and
basted. The arithmetic at line 91 is fine. The transfer is the problem. Say the ratio is
for the fat as a whole, or find a pan-only source that gives an amount.

**Corrected:** line 29: "Use about 1 tablespoon of fat for each sandwich, whether it goes in the pan or on the bread."

### O2. Airy bread always leaks (line 51, Bread)

**Guide:** "Airy bread such as focaccia lets melted cheese seep through and burn."

**Source** (Food Network Kitchen, A Precise Guide…, URL as O1): "your cheese might seep
through the bread and possibly burn in the pan."

**Verdict:** overreach. The source hedges twice, with "might" and "possibly". The guide
states it as certain.

**Corrected:** "Airy bread such as focaccia can let melted cheese seep through and burn in the pan."

### O3. "Don’t press" as a settled answer (line 133, What’s worth arguing about?)

**Guide:** "| Press it flat? | Don’t press the sandwich. Let each side cook undisturbed until it is golden. |"

**Source** (Food Network Kitchen, Grilled Cheese Sandwiches, URL as W1): "Cook, without
pressing, until the bread browns and the cheese is slightly melted, about 5 minutes." And
sources.md on the Food Network guide: "Not stated: pressing the sandwich".

**Verdict:** overreach. One recipe cooks without pressing. No source gives a reason or
argues the point. The row sits under "Every camp makes a good sandwich" but has no camps.

**Corrected:** "| Press it flat? | The one recipe that mentions pressing says not to. Let each side turn golden undisturbed. |"

## Unsourced

### U1. Panini press (lines 62 to 63, Bread, Confused with)

**Guide:** "A panini press closes on the sandwich and cooks both sides at once. A grilled
cheese cooks one side at a time in an open pan."

**Verdict:** unsourced. No source mentions a panini or a press. The open pan is covered
("cook the assembled sandwich uncovered"), but the panini half is not. Either add a public
source on panini presses to `sources.md`, or confuse bread with something the sources
cover. A sourced replacement:

**Corrected:** heading "## Confused with: Focaccia" and text "Focaccia is an airy bread.
Melted cheese might seep through it and burn in the pan." Line 51 then keeps only its first
sentence plus "Sliced white bread is a go-to choice."

### U2. Rye (line 50, Bread)

**Guide:** "Sliced white bread is the usual choice, and sourdough or rye work too."

**Verdict:** unsourced. The source list is "sourdough, multigrain, potato bread, brioche and
challah". Rye is not in it. That list is also a summary, not verbatim. Only "Sliced white
bread is a go-to for grilled cheese sandwiches" is quoted. Re-check the page and keep a
verbatim line for the alternatives.

**Corrected:** "Sliced white bread is the usual choice, and sourdough or potato bread work too."

### U3. Thick slices (line 78, Cheese, Field marks)

**Guide:** "| Watch for | Thick slices melting too slowly |"

**Verdict:** unsourced. No source mentions slice thickness. King Arthur ties heat to slow
cheese: "For cheeses that are slower to melt, use lower heat."

**Corrected:** "| Watch for | Slow-melting cheese on high heat |"

### U4. Butter getting dark (line 98, Butter, Field marks), low

**Guide:** "| Watch for | Butter getting dark too quickly |"

**Verdict:** unsourced, low. No source warns about dark butter. The nearest are the
mayonnaise line ("higher smoke point than butter") and "adjusting the heat as needed". The
Epicurious method browns its butter on purpose, so the warning needs care.

**Corrected:** "| Watch for | Butter browning fast, so turn the heat down |"

## Naming

### N1. "Low" heat on the Heat page (line 111, Heat)

**Guide:** "Low, steady heat gives the cheese time to melt."

**Source** (both Food Network pages): "medium-low heat". How to Boil Water adds
"adjusting the heat as needed".

**Verdict:** naming. Every other line says medium-low. "Low" is a different knob setting,
and "steady" contradicts the source’s advice to adjust.

**Corrected:** "Medium-low heat gives the cheese time to melt. High heat can brown the bread before the cheese melts."

### N2. The target named three ways (lines 14, 43, 71), low

**Guide:** "give the cheese time to melt before the bread burns" (line 14), "before the bread
finishes toasting" (line 43), "before the bread gets too dark" (line 71).

**Source** (Food Network Kitchen, Grilled Cheese Sandwiches, URL as W1): "melt the cheese
completely before the sandwiches are brown".

**Verdict:** naming, low. "Burns" sets the bar lower than the source does. Pick one phrase.

**Corrected:** line 14: "Keep the heat medium-low and give the cheese time to melt before the bread gets too dark."

## Fairness

### F1. The bread camp gets one bare sentence (line 130)

**Guide:** "| Butter the bread or the pan? | Buttering the outside of the bread works. Melting the butter in the pan also works, and the bread soaks it up as it cooks. |"

**Source** (Food Network guide, URL as O1): "spread or brushed on the outside of each
slice". (How to Boil Water, URL as W1): "generously butter the top sides of each slice" and
"Melt another tablespoon of the butter in the skillet".

**Verdict:** fair but thin. The row leaves out that one recipe does both, which is the
friendliest answer to the argument and is sourced.

**Corrected:** "| Butter the bread or the pan? | Spreading butter on the outside of each slice works. Melting it in the pan works too, and the bread soaks it up. One recipe does both. |"

### F2. The opening states pan butter as the only way (lines 14 to 15)

**Guide:** "Butter melted in the pan does the browning."

**Verdict:** fair to state one method, but this sentence answers "What makes a good grilled
cheese?" in general. The main test-kitchen source browns with fat on the bread. A short
second sentence keeps the method and stays fair.

**Corrected:** "Butter melted in the pan does the browning. Butter spread on the bread works too."
