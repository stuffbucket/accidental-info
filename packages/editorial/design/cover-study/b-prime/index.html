Accidental GuidesDesign study, narrowed · 22 October 2026 · The full study

B and B′

Brian’s ask. “The Unigrid band across the top and the option B part grid”, shown next to the existing B, “along with our comparison table evaluation with this narrowed design direction.”

What B′ is

B’s part grid, unchanged, under a fixed black band. The band carries the kicker, the collection, the guide’s number and the collection mark in its top row, and the title in white on its foot. The subtitle sits on white, between the band and the grid.

What the band buys

A series mark you can see at 96 px, a collection you can read from across the room, and a title that stays legible on the site. It doesn’t fix siblings: Claude Code and Claude Desktop still share a pattern, and only the title and number tell them apart.

Recommendation

B′. It keeps everything that made B the pick and adds the shelf and thumbnail identity B was missing, for about 30 more lines in each builder. The costs are ink, print and the Word band, and they’re manageable.

01 · The band

Words in the band, the guide’s parts in the field

Vignelli’s Unigrid puts the title in a black band and gives the rest of the sheet to the map and pictures. B′ does the same: the band holds everything you read, and the field holds the grid, which is the guide’s map.

Rules

Same page, fonts, tiles and data as the study. Only the top of the cover changes.

  1. Band: 150 pt, full bleed, black, the same height on every guide (28% of the page). Fixed, so the shelf has one horizon. It fits a three-line title: Agents, Smoke Detectors and Plugins all clear.
  2. Top row, centred 24 pt below the top edge: An Accidental Guide to… in 9 pt bold white on the left; on the right, the collection name in 7.6 pt grey, the number in 9 pt bold white, then the collection mark, 16 pt, white, on the right margin, where Unigrid puts the Park Service arrowhead.
  3. Title: 27/29 pt bold white, as today, set on the band’s foot: the last baseline always sits 16 pt above it. Short titles leave black above them; that space is the band, not a gap.
  4. Subtitle: 9.4/12.2 pt ink on white, 12 pt below the band, at 80% of the measure.
  5. Grid: B’s grid, unchanged (six 46 pt columns, column heads, two-tone tiles, labels beside), set 4 pt lower than B. The tallest title and subtitle still clear the column-head rule by 18 pt.

How the band was chosen

Four layouts were rendered and compared with B. Two survived; this is the pair.

  • Title inside, not below. With the title below, the band carries only small type, and at 120 px it becomes a black stripe with nothing to read. Inside, the title is the biggest, highest-contrast thing on the cover, which is what a thumbnail needs.
  • First tries collided. A 56 pt band with B’s title spacing ran three-line subtitles into the column heads; a 168 pt band ran every subtitle into them. Setting the grid 4 pt lower and the band at 150 pt clears both.
  • Black, not the collection’s colour. The optional colour band was skipped. The site’s collection colours (blue, yellow, red, green) are family colours too, so a yellow band over yellow Runs tiles would say two things at once, which is the problem the black marks fix. A coloured band would also compete with the grid, which is the only place colour belongs.
  • The marks fit. ■ ▲ ● ◆ read well reversed out of black at 16 pt, and at 96 px they’re still about 5 px, enough to tell a triangle from a circle. The square is the weakest; reversed, it can look like an empty box.
02 · Covers

The same eight guides, B on the left, B′ on the right

The catalog, four AI guides, and one each from the house, the table and games. Real titles, subtitles, parts, families and page numbers, rendered at 324 × 540 pt, 2×.

The sibling test

The study’s open problem with B: Claude Code and Claude Desktop cover the same five parts, so their patterns are identical.

The band doesn’t solve it; nothing that draws only the parts can. It helps in two small ways: the title, the one thing that differs, becomes the strongest element on the cover, and the number (02, 03) moves into a place you can see at a glance. Short titles also change the band’s shape (one line or two on the foot), which tells siblings apart at thumbnail size better than B does. If that isn’t enough, the fix is per guide, not per direction: a one-word tag in the band, or a title that names the product first.

05 · Recommendation

Use B′, with the title in a black band

The study picked B because the cover art is the guide’s own content. B′ keeps that untouched and fixes what B was weakest at: recognising the series, the collection and the title at the size the site shows them. Its costs are a heavier cover, about 30 more lines per builder, and a few output details (a print edge, a Word band, a dark-mode outline). Each of those is small and known; the identity gain isn’t available any other way at no cost per guide.

If warmth wins

If the black reads as too stern for guides meant to calm people down, keep B. B′ is the same build plus the band, so the band can be added later, or tried on the site’s cover images first, without touching the grid.

What Brian needs to decide

  1. B or B′. The band for shelf and thumbnail identity, or the open white cover for warmth.
  2. Title in the band or below it. Recommended inside, as Unigrid does. The thin band with the title on white is the lighter fallback.
  3. Numbers. B′ puts the guide’s number in the band, in plain view. If it stays, numbers are permanent and never reused; if not, the band carries the mark and collection only.
  4. The Word cover. A band inside the margins (simple, recommended) or full bleed (a separate cover section).
  5. Collection marks in black (white on the band), not colour. Unchanged from the study, and B′ depends on it.
How this was made

B′ covers were drawn by b-prime/scripts/bprime.py, which imports the study’s scripts/kit.py and scripts/covers.py (page geometry, TeX Gyre Heros, the two-tone tile, the shelf format and data.json from scripts/data.py) and reuses B’s grid code unchanged apart from its height on the page. Everything is rendered with ReportLab at 324 × 540 pt and rasterised at 2× with pypdfium2, as the study’s covers are. B images are the study’s own, unchanged, from ../img.
