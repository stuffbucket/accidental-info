# Registry policy

## What the registry owns

The registry owns concepts shown as filled tiles in a guide. It does not own every ordinary word in the series.

Each concept record has:

- one plain name;
- one two-letter code;
- one family;
- one owning guide, or a temporary null owner recorded as debt;
- appearances that explain where a tile is shown and whether that guide owns or refers to it.

## Same concept or new concept

Treat two names as the same concept when a newcomer could use one definition in both guides without a hidden exception. Record the product-specific name in the appearance's `entry` field.

Create a new concept when the thing has a different job, lifecycle or owner. Similar spelling is not enough to merge concepts. Different provider vocabulary is not enough to split one.

## Families

- **Ru, Things that run:** active systems and processes.
- **Wr, Things you write down:** stored instructions, state and records.
- **Ck, Things that check:** judgments and mechanisms that examine something.
- **Us, Things an agent uses:** capabilities and resources supplied to a system.
- **Dm, Decisions people make:** policy choices and boundaries selected by people.
- **Nu, Numbers and claims:** measurements, estimates and claims requiring interpretation.

Choose the family from the concept's job in the guide, not from its data type or provider branding.

## Codes

Codes use one uppercase letter followed by one lowercase letter or digit. Prefer recognizable initials, then a recognizable first letter plus a distinguishing letter. Search concepts and `reserved_codes` before choosing.

A retired code remains reserved. Map codes and concept codes are separate in code, but new work avoids collisions so a tile cannot mean two things on the same site.

## Ownership

The owning guide gives the general definition. A reference appearance explains only what is specific to its guide and points to the owner. Ownership moves only as an explicit editorial change across every appearance.
