# The author's feedback, in his words

Collected from his reviews of the first guides. Every rule in this skill comes from one of
these. Give this file to the writer model with every brief.

## On flow and depth

- "This jumps in deep very quickly. The text starts out great and then each concept that
  follows is disjointed definitions strung together to form a paragraph by list. 'Paragraph
  by list' where the entire paragraph could be a list is a bad writing style that models
  often exhibit."
- "we start out with a person and then switch to a team. Stick with a person. A team can
  write down the steps around the agent session. Ok, so what is 'around' the agent session?
  I don't know. A tree? Probably not. We're trying to answer a question: 'what is a software
  factory?' (just be plain like that, not mannered prose - nobody says 'what a software
  factory is' or ends a sentence like that with a verb."
- "there is quite a bit of jargon and assumption. Nobody is speaking to this person."

## On register

- "Phrases such as 'A person can' is very indirect and mannered. This is not a guide for
  Queen Elizabeth II. Use plain and regular English expressions and grammar that do not call
  attention to the writing itself. Nobody cares about how clever the grammar is. They're only
  going to lower their opinion of the guide or give up if it is work or annoying to read."
- "remember no mannered prose and the writing is clear, easy to follow, doesn't put a lot of
  burden on the reader and makes no assumptions that they have a lot of depth in a topic nor
  are they an expert. These are 5 minute reads, tops."
- "make sure that the writing is in my voice. remove all mannered prose ... so the writing
  doesn't call attention to itself."

## On tone, with an example of the register he wants

Not text to copy, an example of the idea and register:

> "If a coding agent is something a person uses to create software with a model, a software
> factory is more of a fancy babysitter that allows people to do something more important,
> like take the dog for a walk. People who use coding agents heavily often end up needing to
> take themselves for a walk because they get lost keeping an eye on the agents."

He added: "something like that, but you have to keep it pocket sized without being staccato."

On a guide opening that claimed what most teams do: "A little bit of tongue in cheek on the
specs guide. most teams don't do any of those things and many people will say that when they
work with a team that their experience has often been that one person does most of the heavy
lifting. Those aren't teams. We're talking about teams." Say plainly which group the guide is
about, with a light, dry touch at most.

## On asides and borrowed frames

- "conceptually this is not the place to introduce a trivia nugget."
- "this is exactly the type of leakage into the guide that I warned against. There is no
  conceptual overlap between birds, species, and software factories. The birding guide was
  one example of structure and pacing, not a literal framework to incorporate into the
  writing explicitly."
- On a terms table that explained what "speakers mean": "Nobody is speaking to this person.
  You are leaking the YouTube format into the writing."

## On sources and quotes

- "It's fine to have those somewhere in the repo as notes that do not go into the guide, but
  don't name check non-public [company] information or direct quotes from people. I didn't see
  any quotes that were particularly noteworthy that would need reference anyway so you just
  drop the reference and keep the idea the quote is trying to convey."

## On length and scope

- "The total time for someone to read through any particular guide from front to back is 5
  minutes or less."
- "There can be more Accidental Guides if they are useful and interesting to people with
  questions and maybe a passing or perhaps begrudging level of interest in the topic. Giving
  them a reason to pick up another guide to read is success. Turning them off or confusing
  them is not."
- "Concepts with a blank box ... tell us that we probably have something in the guide at the
  wrong level of detail. Six concepts is quite a lot... If five concepts will do just as well
  or better than six then consider reducing."
- "The title is a bit wordy can we get that more into balance."

## Two paragraphs he said work

> A software factory is a setup where agent sessions start without a person. The work is
> written down ahead of time as small tasks, and a program starts an agent session for each
> task once the task is ready. Each change then waits for checks and approvals before the
> team accepts it. People still decide what to build and which changes to accept, but they
> don't have to start or watch each session.

> A coding agent can read a task, change files and run tests. A person usually starts each
> agent session, answers questions and checks the result. This works for one task at a time,
> but the person has to keep the work moving.
