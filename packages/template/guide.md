# The question your<br/>reader is asking {#why}

Put the reader’s question in the page title, in their words. Then answer it in two or three short paragraphs: what the thing is, and why anyone would want to know.

Write for someone clever who has never done this before and would quite like to get it over with. Use plain words and short sentences. A joke is welcome if it turns up on its own. Don’t go looking for one.

Finish by naming the parts. Six is the most. Four is fine. Give each one name and use that name every time.

# How do the parts<br/>fit together? {#fit}

Walk through the parts in the order the reader meets them, in two or three paragraphs. Use each part’s name exactly as its own page has it.

If a diagram would help, describe it in a few sentences: what the boxes are and what the arrows mean. The diagram gets drawn when the guide is built.

> [!TIP]
> One practical tip or warning on a page, at most. Delete this box if you don’t have one.

## Field marks

> | Field | Short answer | Detail |
> |---|---|---|
> | When | A short answer first | When the reader meets the part. Change the labels to suit your topic, and use the same ones for every part. |
> | Who | Two to five words | Who does it, brings it or is in charge of it. |
> | Takes | A time or an amount | How long it lasts, or what it costs. |
> | Needs | What has to be ready | Anything the reader must have first. |
> | Can | What it does well | One concrete example. |
> | Cannot | What it won’t do | The mistake people make by expecting it to. |
